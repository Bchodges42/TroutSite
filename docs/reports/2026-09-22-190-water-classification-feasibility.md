# Can the available sources classify all 190 Tennessee waters?

September 22, 2026. Research assessment only; no implementation, outreach, or new final classifications. Read with the [methods audit](2026-09-21-evidence-methods-audit.md), [source acceptance audit](2026-09-22-source-acceptance-and-coverage-audit.md), [unconventional-source discovery](2026-09-22-unconventional-source-discovery.md), and [monitoring follow-up](2026-09-22-local-observers-and-monitoring-followup.md).

## Direct answer

**No, not all 190 as verified, mutually exclusive “year-round trout / seasonal trout / warmwater-no-trout” facts with the material currently in hand.** I do think these sources can support a useful, likely broad map of **documented angling opportunities**, with some waters or reaches explicitly unresolved. The exact supportable count is **unknown**: no independent, claim-by-claim recertification of all 190 against their current reach geometry and sources has yet been performed. I would not convert either the existence of 190 research records or a model's 190 output labels into a coverage percentage.

The decisive choice is the meaning of the warmwater label. **“Warmwater fishing focus”** can be supported by positive bass/panfish/catfish or management evidence and can coexist with seasonal trout. **“No trout”** is an exclusion claim that most public data cannot prove, especially for tributaries, lake arms, seasonal stocking and undocumented catches. Allowing `unresolved` is a requirement for truthful complete *catalog* coverage, even if every water has a page.

The previously published `docs/DATA-SOURCE-COVERAGE.md` reports **91 waters with a stocking source**, but that report's per-water coverage sums to **147 waters** at its earlier snapshot, not the later 190-record habitat set. **Do not cite 91/190 as a measured coverage fraction.** Similarly, a 2021 restoration account for Trail Fork plus a TWRA *plan* to survey it in 2025 do not establish a 2025/2026 population result. Publication/upload dates must not substitute for observation dates.

## Minimum evidence needed by claim

| Proposed visitor-facing claim | Sufficient route for a documented label | What should remain visible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Year-round trout opportunity** | Current, applicable reach-specific fishery assessment (TWRA's explicit 2026 statements suffice where they exist), or well-dated, reach-specific across-season observations paired with management and physical context | Summer limitations, species differences, stocking versus wild status, survey year and any conflicting calendar. A single summer catch or bottom-draw dam is insufficient. |
| **Seasonal stocked trout opportunity** | Correctly joined current program/reach plus its intended fishing window; distinguish scheduled from reported-completed releases. A trustworthy firsthand delivery or dated angling report can be attributed separately | Exact reach, season, stocking date precision, cancellations and the difference between release months and fish survival. |
| **Warmwater fishing focus** | Positive reach-matched warmwater fishery assessment, dated species assemblage or credible seasonal fishing record | Possible mixed/seasonal trout use, and the fact this label does **not** mean trout are biologically absent. |
| **Trout status unresolved** | Missing, conflicting, stale or geometrically ambiguous evidence | Specific missing claim and the best current lead, rather than a low-confidence negative. |

Reservoirs need a **trout fishery/opportunity** label rather than “trout stream,” and lake arms/depth must not inherit a tailwater's temperature. One river name may need multiple reaches. The Caney Fork and Boone cases show why waterbody name matching alone is unsafe.

## What we still need, in order of likely value

1. **A 190-row reach-and-claim adjudication, not another bulk classifier run.** For each catalog row: verify county/drainage, actual mapped line/area and endpoints; list every proposed headline claim, primary source, observation versus publication date, whether it speaks to that reach, source lineage, contradiction and unresolved question. The output would be a measured count of documented/limited/conflicting/unknown waters. This is the missing feasibility census, not website implementation.
2. **Current management and outcome records for positive trout labels.** Seek TWRA's completed stocking-event history beyond the short rolling report; underlying recent electrofishing/creel findings and reach maps for uncertain tailwaters, wild streams, restoration sites and 2025 post-Helene work. Ask specifically whether the 2025 planned surveys in the acquired Coldwater Summit slides were completed and for their results. The [2026 TWRA forecast](https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/dbb92bdf718f4fd7839bf4b08fb82747) already settles some named reaches and should be mined by claim/date before requesting duplicates.
3. **Positive warmwater evidence beyond famous fishing waters.** Acquire TVA's stream fish station inventory and dated species/effort tables, plus usable TDEC/EPA biological-assessment tables and selected NPS/ORNL surveys. Keep sampling design, coordinates, river mile, field date and non-detection caveats. This is more useful for obscure catalog streams than more generic trout search results. Confirm publication/reuse rights separately.
4. **Targeted physical context for genuine disputes.** For a claimed year-round trout reach lacking an explicit current assessment, obtain representative summer temperature **and** dissolved-oxygen records, dam-operation or lake-depth context where relevant, and current fish observations. The USGS compilation is a station-finding/historical check, not a statewide substitute for this work. A logger cannot establish fish presence.
5. **Dated, located supplementary observations.** Guide/shop reports, personal journals, iNaturalist and Fishbrain can expose omissions and seasonal transitions if individual observation date, precise defensible reach, species identification and first-hand provenance are recoverable. The current Fishbrain aggregate extract cannot do this; obtaining more detailed data would also require checking its use rights. Reposts and GBIF-indexed iNaturalist records must be deduplicated.
6. **Owner's product rule for unresolved and mixed waters.** Choose whether the headline promises angling opportunity (recommended), habitat suitability or biological persistence; define how a limited year-round opportunity appears; permit mixed warmwater/seasonal-trout facts and an explicit unresolved state. This is a product decision, not a substitute for evidence.

**Practical judgment:** the available sources are enough to stop guessing and start publishing only positive, traceable opportunity claims once individual rows are adjudicated. They are not enough to certify 190 exclusionary three-way labels today. Some small streams may remain unresolved after reasonable research because no current reach-specific survey, completed release history or reliable observation exists. That is an honest data result, not a failure to fill the spreadsheet.
