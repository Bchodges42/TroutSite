## Evidence-weight adjustment from local full-species sources

The MTFF pages were accessed live on 2026-09-24; individual guide pages show no publication/update date, and the site footer says ©2019. Treat them as useful local angler knowledge that can support or challenge a claim, with lower confidence than a named, dated TWRA survey. The Elk page is especially useful because it separates holdover fish from stream-bred fish and gives access points; its reach is still the Tims Ford tailwater, not the lower Elk. The Nice Mill page exposes a West Fork/East Fork identity mix-up and a stocking-window discrepancy.

For broad scientific fish surveys, omission of trout should now count as **supporting negative evidence**, scaled to the study's actual coverage: full species lists, number and location of sites, season/year, methods and capture suitability, effort, and whether the survey covers the catalog reach. Several well-distributed surveys with methods likely to capture trout can weigh strongly against a trout-positive classification even though they do not prove literal absence. Evans's 42-site New River electrofishing list and the repeated Franklin Harpeth IBI collections are examples where omission is meaningful. A survey limited to one lower site, a mussel survey, a chemistry station, or a partial/taxon-targeted list carries little weight for that inference. Local guides' positive fish lists and direct statements such as “no stream-bred trout known” also count as evidence, clearly tagged as local knowledge and kept separate from proof of a stocking program or naturally reproducing population.
