# Evidence-backed fisheries follow-up review and repairs

Reviewing `feat/evidence-backed-fisheries-20260922` at `6f8c42a` against `origin/main` at `d1e48d1`. This report is being updated as corrections are verified. The repair branch is `codex/evidence-backed-fisheries-repair-20260922`; it is not merged or deployed.

## Confirmed findings

1. **The implementation is materially incomplete.** Its own `owner-corrections-report.json` calls 22 catalog `seasonMonths` values wrong, while the product still uses those values to state whether a trout water is in or out of season. Brush Creek (Cocke) says March–May in the new evidence card and December–February in the legacy field. The committed TWRA schedule confirms five March–May 2026 rows. This is a published contradiction, not merely an owner-side cleanup item.
2. **The claimed zero-absence check is ineffective.** `verify-ledger.mjs` has a ternary that returns `true` for either condition. The ledger uses no `no-trout` enum, but numerous visitor-facing statements make broader claims such as “no trout record exists anywhere.” Those require scoping to the searched data and date, or should be omitted.
3. **The source log cannot verify the committed captures.** Its paths point to uncommitted `evidence-work/captures` paths. The logged 730-point GIS JSON was missing. I re-fetched the live TWRA GIS response on 2026-09-22; its 513,600 bytes and SHA-256 exactly match the logged capture, so it can be restored without inventing provenance. Three other captures match the log. The metadata response was normalized from CRLF to LF by Git; its original-response hash matches the live response but not the committed blob. The HTML mismatch is still unexplained.
4. **Some claim-specific citations are plainly wrong.** Boone Lake's seven species citations all point to USGS station 03486810, whose live page title is “South Fork Holston River at Boone Dam(tw), TN.” That is a tailwater monitoring station, not a Boone Lake fish-species record.
5. **Trail Fork's wild-rainbow wording is stale.** The visitor statement cites a 2017 wild-rainbow plan in present tense, while the 2021 brook-trout transfer account describes removal of rainbows above the waterfall. Its 2026 stocked lower reach can remain documented; the upper-reach population claim must be historical or unresolved.
6. **The old trout calendar was still publishing fabricated current presence.** `troutPresenceNow` treated a researched month range as proof that a water contains trout and called it “freshly stocked” within 21 days of the **first day of a stocking month**, without a completed-release event. `RiverMapPage` passed that into the drawer, which displayed “In season · freshly stocked,” “Out of season,” and absence states beside the new evidence card. The generated calendar was also shipped in the content pack. This directly contradicted the work order's schedule-versus-completed-release rule.
7. **Three of 13 `mixed` headlines lacked water-specific positive warmwater evidence.** McCutcheon Creek's temperature samples were explicitly too sparse; Shelby Farms' warmwater evidence described the *park's other lakes*, not Jones Pond; Stones River had a TWRA winter trout program and a no-summer-survival statement, but no separate water-specific warmwater fishery claim. Their verified trout programs remain seasonal-stocked; mixed is now reserved for waters with both claims.
8. **An evidence quote was deliberately corrupted to bypass validation.** `apply-opportunity.mjs` changed the official local name “Cherokee bass” into “Cherokee' bass” because content validation rejected the phrase anywhere in a record. This changed the quoted source text without solving the underlying typed-species question. The typed species keys already prevent that hybrid name from becoming a `striped-bass` or `spotted-bass` key.
9. **The species-citation pattern affected 16 waters total, not only Boone.** Fifteen more records used USGS hydrology station pages as `speciesEvidence`. Water-specific TWRA pages were re-opened and captured for 13; unsupported species claims were dropped where the pages did not positively name them (and all warmwater target species were withdrawn from Fort Patrick Henry Lake, whose page supplies only a regulations table). Harpeth and Little River smallmouth tags were removed pending a suitable fish-species source. A new content gate rejects hydrology station URLs as fish-species citations.
10. **Legacy map states could still override the evidence ledger.** Six unresolved waters carried a legacy `species: trout` tag (three also had month windows), allowing a trout-condition score or season chip even though the adjudication is unresolved. Three warmwater-focus rivers retained `species: trout`, causing a trout outline despite the warmwater verdict. Removing wrong month windows exposed another path to a trout-condition score on documented seasonal waters. The decision model now lets adjudicated unresolved/warmwater/seasonal verdicts control those presentation states.
11. **The web fallback build still shipped a retired calendar.** Even after the content pack stopped producing `trout-calendar.json`, `copy-pack-fallback.mjs` listed it and left an older copy in `public/content-pack`. Vite then copied that stale JSON into the production bundle. The copy script now removes that exact obsolete file on every build and ships only `streams.json`.
12. **Red River's old catalog note contradicted the new TWRA schedule finding.** It said there was no trout stocking program while the ledger cites Billy Dunlop Park winter stocking on the Clarksville reach. The note and `stockingProgram` flag now acknowledge the park program without claiming river-wide trout presence. Its broad river outline stays warmwater; the reach-scoped opportunity card carries the winter trout finding.

## Repairs made so far

Programmatic waters show “stocking schedule” and withhold trout-condition scoring absent stronger evidence; documented regulatory windows still report their legal open/closed state without saying fish are absent. Focused decision/drawer tests cover this distinction.
- Removed the generated trout-calendar fetch, presence card, class fallback, and content-pack output. A month-label utility remains for describing authored windows. The stocking tab now always labels published fish counts as scheduled.

Trail Fork's 2017/2021 history is separated from 2026 occupancy. Boone Lake species citations now point to TWRA, and unsupported bluegill/channel-catfish typed tags were removed.
- Restored the exact 730-point TWRA GIS response, captured the Boone and Trail Fork source pages plus 13 water-specific TWRA fishery pages, corrected source-log file paths and committed-byte hashes, and added `verify-captures` to content validation.

Evidence-state counts are unchanged.

## TWRA historical completed-release source follow-up (2026-09-24)

- The live [TWRA trout page](https://www.tn.gov/twra/fishing/trout-information-stockings.html) expressly defines its recent-stockings report as a list of Tennessee waters where adult brook, brown, or rainbow trout were recently stocked. It says the report is updated biweekly during stocking season and that each date represents when the waterbody was last stocked, as information is available. The live page said “Report updated as of 9/21/2026”; the live JSON response on 2026-09-24 had 10 rows dated 2026-08-25 through 2026-09-18. This is official completed-release evidence at destination/date level, but the feed is a rolling “last stocked” summary, not a comprehensive event log; it does not give per-row species, fish count, or precise reach.
- I located an [Internet Archive capture of the TWRA page from 2024-06-07](https://web.archive.org/web/20240607134307/https://www.tn.gov/twra/fishing/trout-information-stockings.html) and the contemporaneous [archived TWRA JSON response](https://web.archive.org/web/20240607134309id_/https://www.tn.gov/twra/fishing/trout-information-stockings/_jcr_content/contentFullWidth/tn_panel_348017491_c/content/tn_complex_datatable.exceldriven.json?_=1717767789374). The response contains 54 unique destinations with last-stocked dates from 2024-05-06 through 2024-06-04. It specifically records **Trail Fork Big Creek — 2024-05-20**. This verifies a TWRA-reported stocking at that named destination in 2024; it does not establish what the owner-reported 2025 survey found, current persistence, a specific reach, or species/count for the row.
- A CDX query over the TWRA trout-page subtree found only that one archived `.exceldriven.json` response. Archived page HTML exists on many other dates, but its hidden `wordcloud` block flattens schedule/report terms into an unpaired bag of names and dates; it also includes future scheduled dates in 2026 captures. It is useful for discovery only and cannot safely reconstruct water/date pairs. The absence of additional responses in this indexed URL pattern is not proof that no other copy or differently named endpoint exists.
- Both archived files were retained in `docs/research/2026-09-22-fishery-opportunities/captures/` and added to the capture log with byte counts and SHA-256 hashes. The practical remaining data gap is an agency-maintained completed-release history with event date, water/reach, species, quantity, and a status distinguishing completed from planned/cancelled events; no records request was sent.
