## Confirmed findings

Brush Creek (Cocke) says March–May in the new evidence card and December–February in the legacy field. The committed TWRA schedule confirms five March–May 2026 rows. This is a published contradiction, not merely an owner-side cleanup item.

The ledger uses no `no-trout` enum, but numerous visitor-facing statements make broader claims such as “no trout record exists anywhere.” Those require scoping to the searched data and date, or should be omitted.
3. **The source log cannot verify the committed captures.** Its paths point to uncommitted `evidence-work/captures` paths. The logged 730-point GIS JSON was missing. I re-fetched the live TWRA GIS response on 2026-09-22; its 513,600 bytes and SHA-256 exactly match the logged capture, so it can be restored without inventing provenance. Its 2026 stocked lower reach can remain documented; the upper-reach population claim must be historical or unresolved.
6. **The old trout calendar was still publishing fabricated current presence.** `troutPresenceNow` treated a researched month range as proof that a water contains trout and called it “freshly stocked” within 21 days of the **first day of a stocking month**, without a completed-release event. `RiverMapPage` passed that into the drawer, which displayed “In season · freshly stocked,” “Out of season,” and absence states beside the new evidence card. The generated calendar was also shipped in the content pack. This directly contradicted the work order's schedule-versus-completed-release rule.
7. **Three of 13 `mixed` headlines lacked water-specific positive warmwater evidence.** McCutcheon Creek's temperature samples were explicitly too sparse; Shelby Farms' warmwater evidence described the *park's other lakes*, not Jones Pond; Stones River had a TWRA winter trout program and a no-summer-survival statement, but no separate water-specific warmwater fishery claim. 8. **An evidence quote was deliberately corrupted to bypass validation.** `apply-opportunity.mjs` changed the official local name “Cherokee bass” into “Cherokee' bass” because content validation rejected the phrase anywhere in a record. This changed the quoted source text without solving the underlying typed-species question. The typed species keys already prevent that hybrid name from becoming a `striped-bass` or `spotted-bass` key.
9. **The species-citation pattern affected 16 waters total, not only Boone.** Fifteen more records used USGS hydrology station pages as `speciesEvidence`. Water-specific TWRA pages were re-opened and captured for 13; unsupported species claims were dropped where the pages did not positively name them (and all warmwater target species were withdrawn from Fort Patrick Henry Lake, whose page supplies only a regulations table). Harpeth and Little River smallmouth tags were removed pending a suitable fish-species source. A new content gate rejects hydrology station URLs as fish-species citations.
10. **Legacy map states could still override the evidence ledger.** Six unresolved waters carried a legacy `species: trout` tag (three also had month windows), allowing a trout-condition score or season chip even though the adjudication is unresolved. Three warmwater-focus rivers retained `species: trout`, causing a trout outline despite the warmwater verdict. Removing wrong month windows exposed another path to a trout-condition score on documented seasonal waters. The decision model now lets adjudicated unresolved/warmwater/seasonal verdicts control those presentation states.
11. **The web fallback build still shipped a retired calendar.** Even after the content pack stopped producing `trout-calendar.json`, `copy-pack-fallback.mjs` listed it and left an older copy in `public/content-pack`. Vite then copied that stale JSON into the production bundle. The copy script now removes that exact obsolete file on every build and ships only `streams.json`.
12. **Red River's old catalog note contradicted the new TWRA schedule finding.** It said there was no trout stocking program while the ledger cites Billy Dunlop Park winter stocking on the Clarksville reach. The note and `stockingProgram` flag now acknowledge the park program without claiming river-wide trout presence. ## Lower Elk: direct fish-survey evidence at the Tennessee–Alabama line (2026-09-24)

The official [Geological Survey of Alabama Open-File Report 0506](https://www.ogb.state.al.us/img/Ecosystems/pdf/OFR_0506.pdf), by Thomas E. Shepard, Patrick E. O'Neil, Stuart W. McGregor, and Maurice F. Mettee, is titled *Survey of the Elk River System in Alabama for Fish Species of Moderate to Highest Conservation Concern: Report of Results for 2004*. Although the survey focused on conservation-concern fish in the basin, it includes a complete list of collected fishes and two stations the authors explicitly place on the Elk main stem in **Giles County, Tennessee** near the border:

| Station | Reported location / coordinates | Collection events | Gear and Appendix A yield |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ER-23, RM 34 | Winding Stair Bluff; 34°59′57.6″N, 87°01′47.8″W | July 29 and Aug. 16, 2004 | 10-ft seine; second visit also backpack electrofishing. 15 species / 55 individuals, then 30 / 179. |
| ER-24, RM 34.5 | Elk River shoal; 35°00′01.0″N, 87°01′31.1″W | July 29, Aug. 2, and Aug. 16, 2004 | 10-ft seine + backpack electrofishing each visit; July 29 also boat electrofishing. 46 / 777, 21 / 231, and 35 / 679. |

Table 1 gives the locality, dates, coordinates, and methods (PDF pp. 10–11; printed pp. 8–9). The Appendix A station tables report the counts above (PDF pp. 54–59; printed pp. 52–57), and Table 2 reports the system's 91 collected species (PDF p. 15; printed p. 13). The Appendix A / Table 2 fish list contains no salmonid or trout taxon. This is good supporting negative evidence for a **resident trout community at the two border-area stations during repeated summer sampling**: the work used methods suited to collecting a broad stream-fish community, and reports dozens of species and hundreds of individuals from the Tennessee stations. The report's only explicit trout mention is contextual: cold discharge from Tims Ford Dam supports a stocked-trout fishery upstream, about 100 river miles from the Alabama state line (PDF p. 9; printed p. 7). That passage does not describe the lower Elk.

Limits matter. These five visits cover the border-end stations, not the full reach upstream to Prospect; they were all in late July/August and cannot rule out winter releases or a rare/isolated trout, and they are from 2004. This study is not a TWRA stocking record. There is also a source-internal sample-count discrepancy: the abstract says 56 samples, while the Methods section says 57 samples at the same 47 stations (PDF pp. 3 and 8; printed pp. 1 and 6). The two station details and their Appendix A totals are clear despite that systemwide discrepancy.

The report cites an older, potentially valuable source: Thomas S. Jandebeur, *A study of the fishes of the Elk River drainage in Tennessee and Alabama* (University of Alabama master's thesis, 1972, 154 pp.). The 2004 report says Jandebeur surveyed the **entire drainage** from 1967–1971 and recorded 104 species. I searched the University of Alabama Institutional Repository's public discovery API by author and title, the Internet Archive catalog, Open Library, WorldCat, and web search; the cited Elk thesis did not surface as a readable digitized item. The UA repository does contain another fish thesis by Jandebeur, on the North River, which is not the Elk study. Treat the Elk thesis as a **specific, promising library-holdings lead**, not as reviewed evidence. University of Alabama Libraries/Graduate School and TWRA's historical fish-collection records are likely custodians; no one was contacted.

## Additional Water Quality Portal fish-tissue search (2026-09-24)

The `Biological` results at these stations were kick-net / BioRecon / other benthic-invertebrate samples, not fish-community lists; their omission of trout is therefore **not** negative fish evidence. The separate `Tissue` results provide useful dated fish captures from electrofishing, but are contaminant samples and are taxon-selective, not complete fish inventories:

- **Powell River:** [site TNW000004938](https://www.waterqualitydata.us/data/Result/search?siteid=TDECWR_WQX-TNW000004938&sampleMedia=Tissue&mimeType=csv&zip=no), at 36.54151, -83.63091, contains channel catfish, smallmouth bass, and largemouth bass tissue records spanning 1991–2023.
- **Pigeon River:** [site TNW000004782](https://www.waterqualitydata.us/data/Result/search?siteid=TDECWR_WQX-TNW000004782&sampleMedia=Tissue&mimeType=csv&zip=no), at 35.95020, -83.17900, has tissue records dated 1994–2022 for channel catfish, spotted bass, common carp, redbreast sunfish, largemouth bass, smallmouth bass, rock bass, and walleye. This independently supports a long-running warmwater fish assemblage at that station; it does not provide a complete list or show trout absence.
- **Lower Elk:** [site TNW000002167](https://www.waterqualitydata.us/data/Result/search?siteid=TDECWR_WQX-TNW000002167&sampleMedia=Tissue&mimeType=csv&zip=no), at 35.02690, -86.94750, has electrofishing/tissue records dated 1989–2023 for channel catfish, bass, and freshwater drum. Match this point against the catalog's Prospect-to-state-line geometry before assigning it to that exact reach.
- **Little Pigeon main channel:** [sites TNW000003852](https://www.waterqualitydata.us/data/Result/search?siteid=TDECWR_WQX-TNW000003852&sampleMedia=Tissue&mimeType=csv&zip=no) and [TNW000008179](https://www.waterqualitydata.us/data/Result/search?siteid=TDECWR_WQX-TNW000008179&sampleMedia=Tissue&mimeType=csv&zip=no) record smallmouth bass tissue samples in 2019. These sparse records add warmwater species context but cannot replace Doosey's main-stem survey or support a trout non-detection.
- **East Fork Stones River:** the earlier [TNW000002137 results](https://www.waterqualitydata.us/data/Result/search?siteid=TDECWR_WQX-TNW000002137&sampleMedia=Tissue&mimeType=csv&zip=no) include 2018 catfish and bass samples; [TNW000008453](https://www.waterqualitydata.us/data/Result/search?siteid=TDECWR_WQX-TNW000008453&sampleMedia=Tissue&mimeType=csv&zip=no) adds spotted and largemouth bass in 2022. These remain downstream / station-specific tissue samples, not a complete upper-fork survey.

This source lane is useful for verified fish occurrence and for locating sampled stations, and it may lead to underlying TDEC/TVA project reports. Do **not** turn a missing trout row in fish-tissue or benthic data into an absence claim.

An archive search also surfaced UT's [“Pigeon Snorkel Survey”](https://trace.tennessee.edu/handle/20.500.14382/12532) (2013), but the record's attachment is a photo, with no fish list, survey stations, or effort in the available metadata. It is a lead to the existence of field activity only, not classification evidence.

Additional access finding: the live [TWRA Surveys and Species / Statewide Fish Stocking portal](https://twraapps.tn.gov/TSAS/pages/sfs/default.aspx) advertises a historical **1990–2016 coldwater stocking export**. Its export URL redirects to a login page when opened without credentials, so it is a precise agency records-request target, not a public source we can claim to have analyzed. The catalog's named reach must be matched to site coordinates before using any export row.

Rejected lead: [Appalachian Outdoors' New River fishing page](https://www.appoutdoorsco.com/new-river-fly-fishing) calls the Scott County river a smallmouth/musky fishery, but also asserts unspecified “seasonal trout” and “stocked in some sections” without dates, named reaches, or agency source. It is not needed for the warmwater finding because Evans's geolocated field survey is stronger, and it must not be used to establish a trout program.

## Evidence-weight adjustment from local full-species sources

The MTFF pages were accessed live on 2026-09-24; individual guide pages show no publication/update date, and the site footer says ©2019. Treat them as useful local angler knowledge that can support or challenge a claim, with lower confidence than a named, dated TWRA survey. The Elk page is especially useful because it separates holdover fish from stream-bred fish and gives access points; its reach is still the Tims Ford tailwater, not the lower Elk. The Nice Mill page exposes a West Fork/East Fork identity mix-up and a stocking-window discrepancy.

For broad scientific fish surveys, omission of trout should now count as **supporting negative evidence**, scaled to the study's actual coverage: full species lists, number and location of sites, season/year, methods and capture suitability, effort, and whether the survey covers the catalog reach. Several well-distributed surveys with methods likely to capture trout can weigh strongly against a trout-positive classification even though they do not prove literal absence. Evans's 42-site New River electrofishing list and the repeated Franklin Harpeth IBI collections are examples where omission is meaningful. A survey limited to one lower site, a mussel survey, a chemistry station, or a partial/taxon-targeted list carries little weight for that inference. Local guides' positive fish lists and direct statements such as “no stream-bred trout known” also count as evidence, clearly tagged as local knowledge and kept separate from proof of a stocking program or naturally reproducing population.
