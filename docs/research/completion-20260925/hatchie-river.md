# Hatchie River (West TN) — warmwater classification log

Retrieval date for all web sources: 2026-09-25. Research only; no agency/business/author contact.

Water: Hatchie River, West Tennessee (Hardeman/McNairy/Chester/Madison/Haywood/Tipton counties), last major unchannelized West TN river; longest free-flowing tributary of the lower Mississippi; Tennessee State Scenic River in its entirety; main stem of the Hatchie (the "Hatchie River" of the TWRA West TN crappie-rule waters list).

## Warmwater / fishery evidence

1. **USFWS — Hatchie National Wildlife Refuge, "Fishing" page** (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service; page undated, retrieved 2026-09-25). https://www.fws.gov/refuge/hatchie/visit-us/activities/fishing
   Fields: refuge fishing open year-round (daylight only; some areas closed Nov 15–Mar 15; electric trolling motors only). Fishable waters: "10 oxbow lakes, four borrow pit lakes, three lakes, numerous sloughs and creeks," and fishable waters along the Hatchie River ("a Tennessee Scenic River"). Species named: largemouth bass, bluegill, crappie, catfish (Goose and Quail Hollow Lakes carry a 14-inch bass minimum). **Trout: not mentioned at all.**
   Warmwater evidence: YES (federal agency describes a bass/bluegill/crappie/catfish fishery on the river and its oxbows). Type: agency page, undated. Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH. Establishes: warmwater fishery on the river corridor and the river's Scenic River status per federal agency. Not established: main-stem sampling data (page is regulation/visitor info).

2. **Jeff Samsel, "Four Unsung Tennessee Tributaries For Catfish," Game & Fish, published 2010-10-04** (observation/quote content reflects 2010 TWRA Region I sampling guidance). https://www.gameandfishmag.com/editorial/fishing_catfish-fishing_tn_0705_01/191698 (full article opened 2026-09-25)
   Fields: Tim Broadbent, TWRA Region I fisheries biologist: the Hatchie "supports an outstanding catfish population." Channel catfish dominate anglers' catches; flatheads in "timber-tangled holes"; blue catfish move up into the lower river from the Mississippi. Public boat ramps at US 70/79, Highway 54, and US 51 crossings. Other waters in the article: Duck, Buffalo, Holston, French Broad rivers (all warmwater).
   Warmwater evidence: YES. Type: dated outdoor press quoting a named TWRA fisheries biologist. Confidence: HIGH. Establishes: TWRA-managed, agency-documented warmwater catfish fishery on the Hatchie main stem, with named public access (dated 2010).

3. **TWRA 2026 regulations via eRegulations — "Exceptions to Statewide Regulations," Last Updated: September 15, 2026.** https://www.eregulations.com/tennessee/fishing/exceptions-to-statewide-regulations (page opened 2026-09-25)
   Fields (verbatim): "Forked Deer, Hatchie, Loosahatchie, Obion and Wolf Rivers (includes tributaries)" — "Crappie: 30 per day, no length limit."
   Warmwater evidence: YES — the Hatchie main stem is a named member of TWRA's West TN rivers crappie-exception list ("Hatchie River crappie rule" waters list member confirmed verbatim, current 2026). Type: current agency regulation (dated Sept 15, 2026). Confidence: HIGH.

6. **Context — Scenic River status (dated):** Tennessee Scenic Rivers Act of 1968 (T.C.A. Title 11); USDA SCS "Hatchie River Basin land treatment plan" notes the TN Department of Conservation organized an interagency task force in **December 1975** for a "Hatchie Scenic River Project" (https://upload.wikimedia.org — USDA document scan). Hatchie River Conservancy (founded November 2023), https://www.hatchieriver.org/ (opened 2026-09-25): the Hatchie is "the longest free-flowing tributary of the lower Mississippi River," harbors "the largest forested floodplain remaining in Tennessee," and is "the only river in the state that is designated as a State Scenic River [in its entirety]."
   Warmwater evidence: indirect (unchannelized warmwater lowland river symbolism). Confidence: HIGH for status claims; note Conservancy page is undated beyond founding.

## Verdict / recommendation
**Warmwater CONFIRMED.** The Hatchie River main stem is a TWRA-managed warmwater river: agency biologist-documented catfish fishery (2010, dated), named member of the current (2026) crappie-exception waters list, USFWS-documented bass/bluegill/crappie/catfish fishery across the refuge corridor, and a species-rich warmwater fish fauna (~85–100+ species; Starnes c. 1972; Keck & Etnier 2005). Zero trout evidence in any category: no schedule row, no NAS occurrence, no GBIF occurrence (controls passed), no trout mention in any fishery description. Suggested label: "warmwater (documented catfish/bass/crappie fishery; State Scenic River; no trout evidence, any category)."

Gaps: full text of Keck & Etnier 2005 and Starnes thesis not retrieved (paywall/repository blocks) — species counts are search-snippet level; TDEC WQMP fish lists not pulled; TML briefing-book "100 species / 11 catfish" claim not opened directly (snippet only).
