# EMORY RIVER (Morgan County, TN; lower river = Roane Co / Watts Bar arm)
Research log — internal classification research only. Retrieval date for all sources: 2026-09-25.
Reach: three sub-reaches kept distinct — (1) Emory River NPS WSR segment (Obed confluence to Nemo Bridge, inside OBRI, Morgan Co); (2) upper/middle Emory (Cumberland/Morgan headwaters, Oakdale, Hwy 299); (3) lower Emory (Harriman to mouth, Roane Co; Watts Bar embayment; the Dec 2008 TVA Kingston ash spill entered the Emory via Swan Pond Creek Embayment at Emory RM ~2-3).
Known trout item in the broader drainage: Flat Fork Creek (Frozen Head SP, Wartburg) — stocked with rainbow trout (see S5); it drains the Frozen Head area toward the Little Emory/Emory system, so the system-wide "known Flat Fork item" sits in this watershed, not on the Emory main stem.

## SOURCES

### S1. Benck et al. 2017 — NPS Natural Resource Condition Assessment, Obed WSR (NRR-2017/1554) — Emory segment (within OBRI)
- Retrieved 2026-09-25: https://npshistory.com/publications/obed/nrr-2017-1554.pdf
- Fish passages (pp. 145-146): (a) "Russ (2006) sampled just one location on the Emory River within OBRI boundaries during the summer of 2005, and identified 29 different species... the only OBRI location where Russ (2006) found logperch (Percina caprodes), spotted bass (Micropterus punctulatus), ashy darter (Etheostoma cinereum), and gilt darter (Percina evides). Two non-native species were documented: the redbreast sunfish and the striped shiner." (b) "Thirty-four fish species were documented by Scott (2010b) during 2004 and 2006 sampling at five Emory River sites; four sites were in close proximity but different sampling methods were used at each (e.g., gill net vs. minnow trap)... the Emory was the only OBRI watershed found to support the muskellunge, channel catfish, and logperch."
- Whole-document: "trout" = 0. Warmwater sportfish documented: spotted bass, muskellunge, channel catfish, drum, sunfishes.
- Type: method-documented dated surveys (2004/2005/2006; gill-net + minnow-trap + seine) with full species lists omitting trout. Confidence: high (weighted).

### S2. Rigg, D.K., Wacksman, M.N., Iannuzzi, J., Baker, T.F., Adams, M., Greeley, M.S. Jr. 2015 — "Assessing Ecological Risks to the Fish Community from Residual Coal Fly Ash in Watts Bar Reservoir, Tennessee," Integrated Environmental Assessment and Management, DOI 10.1002/ieam.1588 (ARCADIS/TVA/ORNL)
- Retrieved 2026-09-25 full text: https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1286816
- Fields: post-Kingston-spill (Dec 2008) fish-community risk assessment for the Emory/Clinch/Tennessee River reaches. Observations: (a) initial fish kill (~200-300 fish observed by TVA and TWRA within a week) composed of threadfin shad, freshwater drum, smallmouth buffalo, largemouth bass, sunfish (Lepomis spp.) — all warmwater; (b) "the fish community at the Emory River location that was added after the ash spill consistently received 'Good' RFAI ratings [TVA Reservoir Fish Assemblage Index, 12-60 scale]"; (c) TVA "spring sport fish survey" + RFAI multi-year surveys — "fish assemblage in the ash-affected reaches of the Emory and Clinch Rivers to be comparable to similar locations in the Tennessee Valley"; overall risk "negligible"; (d) reproduction indices measured on bluegill at Emory River Mile 8.0 and 3.0 (GSI, fecundity, atresia, vitellogenic oocytes) and largemouth bass — i.e., warmwater species monitored on the Emory proper, by river mile.
- Zero trout/salmon mentions in the paper (text-mined). Method: agency-scale multi-year standardized surveys. Confidence: high.
- Establishes: Emory (post-spill) monitored as a warmwater fish community at RM 2.5-8; sportfish survey infrastructure (TVA/TWRA) treats it as warmwater reservoir-transition habitat.

## SPECIES FOUND vs OMITTED
- Found: within-park segment: 29 spp. (Russ 2006) / 34 spp. (Scott 2010b) incl. muskellunge, spotted bass, channel catfish, logperch, gilt + ashy darters; lower Emory: TVA RFAI "Good" warmwater assemblage, bluegill/largemouth/redear/white crappie/channel catfish health studies; Watts Bar arm: striped bass, black crappie, walleye, FLMB stockings, paddlefish run, muskellunge "preferred habitat in the... Emory Rivers" (TWRA).
- Omitted: all Salmonidae on every dated agency list and in the entire ash-spill fish literature.

## VERDICT
Warmwater-focus CONFIRMED and strengthened. The strongest fisheries record for any water in this study — the post-Kingston-spill monitoring complex (TVA sport-fish surveys, RFAI assemblage ratings, ORNL sentinel-species health studies, the IEAM risk assessment) — uniformly documents and manages the Emory as a warmwater river/reservoir-transition fishery; the NPS-segment surveys (2004-2006) found 29-34 species with zero salmonids; TWRA's muskie/paddlefish/stripped-bass/crappie programs treat it as warmwater; the drainage's only trout stocking is the Flat Fork headwater program at Wartburg. No trout claims to test beyond GBIF off-reach iNat pins (leads only).
Gaps: pre-2008 TVA RFAI tables for the Emory not independently retrieved (Rigg 2015 summarizes them); no TWRA creel report for the Emory located.
