# Obey River (Dale Hollow Dam tailwater), Clay County — trout-evidence research log

Water: **Obey River / Dale Hollow tailwater** — Dale Hollow Dam (Clay Co., near Celina) downstream ~12 km (7.5 river mi) to the Cumberland River confluence. USACE-owned dam (Nashville District); trout stocked by TWRA (Region 3, Crossville) with USFWS Dale Hollow National Fish Hatchery (on the tailwater below the dam). Hatchery Creek (small tributary below the hatchery) also stocked.
Reach/coordinates: TWRA stocking point 36.5368, -85.4573 ("Dale Hollow Dam Recreation Area"); Tailwater_Trout line 39,056.87 **feet** (layer units esriFeet) = 7.40 mi = 11.9 km — NOTE: the task's "39,057 m" is a unit misread; the layer stores feet, and 7.4 mi matches TWRA's own "about 12km (7.5 river miles)" description. Retrieval date for all sources: **2026-09-25** (Wayback snapshots dated individually below). No agency/business/author/angler contact. No catalog edits.

### A4. TWRA "Management Plan for the Obey River Trout Fishery 2008-2014" (KEY SOURCE)
- Title/author/org: "Management Plan for the Obey River Trout Fishery 2008-2014", James Travis Scott, Jack Swearengin, Walter Scott Webb, D.W. Young — Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency, **January 2008** (Fisheries Report series; region Region 3)
- Retrieval 2026-09-25 via Wayback capture 2018-01-12: https://web.archive.org/web/20180112212428if_/https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/twra/documents/Obey_River_Trout_Fishery_2008-2014.pdf
- Observation dates inside: creel surveys 1995 (Bettoli 1996) and 2001 (Bettoli 2002); electrofishing/stocking series 1999–2006 (Figs 1–7); DO studies Young 1998; TVA turbine-venting tests Sept 1999 (Carter 2000); temps Young 1996.
- Key fields:
  - **Goal: "To maintain a quality, year-round fishery for rainbow and brown trout within the Dale Hollow Tailwater."** (TWRA's own words)
  - Recommendation: "**Continue to stock 57,000, 9 to 10 inch rainbow trout throughout the year**"; "Continue to stock 1,000 8-inch brown trout each spring"; "**In 2008, start stocking brook trout (northern hatchery strains)** … stock 500 to 1,000 annually."
  - Regulations: "Maintain statewide fishing limits for trout: a daily creel of 7 trout, with no bait or size restrictions"; "size restrictions are not biologically warranted" (rainbows = put-and-take).
  - Physical: dam built 1945, 3 turbines, ~1,800 cfs each, 25 cfs continuous min flow; tailwater 12 km / 7.5 mi to Cumberland R.; 90% bank / 8% wade / 1% float; most pressure upper 3 km; water temps 42–56 °F year-round (Young 1996); fall DO historically critical (<2 mg/L documented 1998; turbine-venting fix on unit 1 in 1999); oligotrophic reservoir → nutrient-poor tailwater.
  - Biology/holdover: rainbows "essentially limited to put-and-take" (few survive >14 in despite consistent stocking); **browns "have greater potential to grow and survive"; since 1999, 58% of electrofished browns >12 in; browns "surviving… convert to natural food sources," Wr up to >100%; "the few that do survive multiple years provide anglers an opportunity to catch large trout"** — brown holdover demonstrated; no evidence of natural reproduction cited for any species.
  - Economics/anglers: $1.7M/yr value (Williams & Bettoli 2003); catch rates 1.03 trout/hr (1995) and 1.19 (2001); 67% of anglers harvest; 86% bait use; Hatchery Creek used by 28–54% of anglers; 20,000 anglers/yr objective.
- Type: government management plan (agency synthesis of creel + electrofishing data). Confidence: HIGH. Establishes: year-round goal + stocking rates + species + regs + holdover character. Does NOT establish: month-by-month stocking records 2003–2015 (not published in this document beyond the series figures).

## C. Holdover / reproduction / fishery character
- Brown trout holdover/growth: multiple-year survival, 58% of electrofished browns >12 in, high Wr (2008–2014 plan, §Discussion, Figs 3/5/7 — data 1999–2006). Rainbow: put-and-take, low multi-year survival.
- Natural reproduction: none documented for Obey; 2017-2027 statewide plan attributes significant natural reproduction to South Holston and Wilbur browns only. NAS "locally established" 1993 label for O. mykiss tailwater is a database status (consistent with a sustained stocked fishery), not evidence of wild reproduction.
- Winter fishery character: creel surveys (1995, 2001) ran the full season with 1.0–1.19 trout/hr catch rates; completed winter releases (Dec–Feb series above); water temps 42–56 °F year-round (Young 1996 in A4).
- Habitat constraint (context): fall DO historically below 2 mg/L pre-1999; unit-1 turbine venting improved it; noted as the tailwater's main limiter, not temperature.

## Contradictions / cautions
1. **Species-list drift across TWRA's own products (2026):** layers say brook+brown+rainbow; 2026 JSON says Rainbow+Brown; live page regulation line says "Brook, Rainbow". The 3-species list (with brook since 2008) is the best-supported; brown omission in the live-page text line and brook omission in the JSON look like editorial inconsistencies, not program changes — but a 2026 brown-or-brook discontinuation cannot be fully excluded from public data.
2. **Task brief said line length "39,057 m"** — layer units are feet (39,056.87 ft = 11.9 km); TWRA's own plan says 12 km/7.5 mi. Catalog should read feet or convert to km.
3. **Schedule coverage gap 2003–2015:** the only statewide weekly schedules from that era exclude ALL tailwaters; "no Obey row" in sched03–15 and in Complete.pdf 2020–2025 must not be read as no stocking (completed-release feeds and plans prove continuity).
4. **Completed-release feeds are "last stocked" lists updated bi-weekly** — they prove presence, not month-by-month absence; months without a captured date (notably Apr, Jun, Oct) lack a direct dated capture in this pass.
5. **2024 archive window** (Wayback JSON) covers only 05/06–06/04/2024 → exactly one Dale Hollow TW row (05/31/2024) exists by construction, not by scarcity.
