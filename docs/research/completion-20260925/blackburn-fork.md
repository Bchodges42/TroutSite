# Blackburn Fork (Jackson/Putnam Counties, TN) — trout-evidence research log

Water: **Blackburn Fork River** ("Blackburn Fork State Scenic River") — Highland Rim stream of the Upper Cumberland; headwaters near Cookeville (formed by East and West Blackburn Fork creeks, Putnam Co.), flowing north through **Cummins Falls State Park** (75-ft Cummins Falls) to its mouth at the **Roaring River**, Jackson Co. (36.34735, -85.56227). Ledger verdict under test: **warmwater-focus**.
Reach/coordinates: designated segment "from the county road at Cummings Mill downstream 1.5 mi" (Class I) + "1.5 mi below Cummings Mill … to its meeting with the Roaring River" (Class II, 13.2 mi) — mouth 36.34735/-85.56227 (GNIS/Wikipedia); park address 390 Cummins Falls Lane, Cookeville TN.
Retrieval date for all sources: **2026-09-25**. Research only; no contact; no catalog edits.

**RECOMMENDATION (bottom line): warmwater-focus — CONFIRMED.** Zero trout stocking is planned or recorded anywhere in the TWRA schedule corpus 2009–2026 (no "Blackburn" row of any kind statewide), none in the ArcGIS stocking layers, no trout in museum lots or iNaturalist; the stream's documented fishery is smallmouth bass + rock bass (TDEC scenic-river page; TN State Parks ranger-led event). Identity: this is the Jackson Co. scenic river; do NOT conflate with (a) East Blackburn Fork Creek / West Blackburn Fork Creek (its own Putnam Co. headwater forks), or (b) Blackburn Fork of the Locust Fork/Black Warrior system, Blount County, AL (museum lots in GBIF). Confidence: HIGH.

### A2. TDEC — Tennessee Scenic Rivers, "Blackburn Fork" page — designation + smallmouth fishery
- Org: Tennessee Department of Environment & Conservation; https://www.tn.gov/environment/natural-areas/tn-scenic-rivers/blackburn-fork.html (WebFetch 2026-09-25).
- Fields: **designated April 4, 1968** (Tennessee Scenic Rivers Act); **14.7 mi total** — Class I Natural River Area 1.5 mi ("from the county road at Cummings Mill downstream one and one-half (1.5) miles"), Class II Pastoral River Area 13.2 mi (thence to the Roaring River); Jackson County; "Highland Rim of the Upper Cumberland Plateau"; key feature Cummins Falls ("eighth largest waterfall in Tennessee") within Cummins Falls State Park; fishery statement: "**Smallmouth bass**, a prized catch for anglers wading the river or fishing from rocky banks."
- Establishes: primary-source designation facts + agency-documented warmwater (smallmouth) fishery. Confidence: HIGH.

### A5. Corroborating geography/identity
- Wikipedia "Blackburn Fork River" (retrieved 2026-09-25; redirects from "Blackburn Fork State Scenic River"): mouth at Roaring River 36.34735/-85.56227; "major tributary of the Roaring River"; source = confluence of West Blackburn Fork Creek and East Blackburn Fork Creek (north-central Putnam Co.); "Hiking, swimming, and fishing are popular activities"; Cummins Falls within Cummins Falls State Park.
- Roaring River context: Blackburn Fork water reaches the Cumberland via Roaring River → Cordell Hull Reservoir arm (Roaring River arm) — the entire drainage is below Cordell Hull Dam's coldwater influence; no tailwater.
