### S3. TWRA Reservoir Report 2022 — Boone chapter (dated agency survey; CORNERSTONE)
- Title/org: "2022 Reservoir Report", TWRA Fisheries (Boone Reservoir chapter, report pp. 333–344). Observation year 2022 (with 2013–2022 data tables); report archived in Wayback 2024-08-20. Retrieval 2026-09-25.
- URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20240820015357id_/https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/twra/documents/fishing/TWRA_Reservoir_Report_2022.pdf
- Verbatim keys: "Boone Reservoir water levels were held at 10ft below winter pool elevations (1350–1355 feet above mean sea level) from 2014–2021 for dam repairs. Boone Reservoir is now back to its normal operating schedule in 2022." "Boone Reservoir was stocked with 50,000 F-1 hybrid largemouth bass, for the second consecutive year, in early June 2022… first generation cross between a Northern strain largemouth and a Florida strain largemouth… The plan is to stock advanced F-1 largemouth bass fingerlings into the reservoir for 3 years at a rate of 50,000 per year… We are hopeful… that Boone Reservoir will produce some trophy largemouth bass." LMB electrofishing CPUE 64.0 fish/h ("well above average"); SMB CPUE 11.3 (below average, but ≥15-in catch rates about average); black crappie about average. Creel: total effort 116,902 angler-hours (2022), fishery value $456,580; black-bass effort 54,264 hrs. Striped-bass winter gill-netting not conducted 2022 (shad/Morone gill-netting Sept 2022).
- TROUT CHECK: the full chapter (pp. 333–344) contains ZERO occurrences of "trout" or any salmonid name (programmatic grep). Species tables: largemouth, smallmouth, spotted bass, white crappie, black crappie; angling tables for black bass/crappie/catfish/stripers.
- Type: agency monitoring report (observed). Confidence: high. Establishes: (a) the drawdown era 2014–2021 and refill to normal operations in 2022, dated; (b) an active F1 largemouth trophy program in the refill era; (c) documented warmwater survey effort; (d) zero trout in TWRA's own reservoir reporting.

## 3. Warmwater evidence summary
1. Agency profile: bass/stripers/hybrids/catfish predominant; active stocking of blue cat (since 1992), stripers/hybrids (since 1972), black crappie (S1).
2. Dated agency surveys: drawdown 2014–2021 → normal ops 2022 (S3, S4); bass fishery carried CPUE ~63–64 fish/h across the drawdown and refill (S3, S4).
3. Dated warmwater program news: F1 largemouth 50k/yr 2021–2023 (150k total, agency-confirmed June 2023) + stripers/hybrids/crappie plans (S5–S7).
4. Zero trout: no lake row in 2026 schedule (S2); zero trout mentions in 2016+2022 reservoir report chapters (S3, S4); no section in the 2026 trout forecast (S9); trout occurrences = Watauga-arm tailwater migrants only (S8).

## 4. Contradictions / tension points
1. The regs-table trout line (7-creel, 16–22 PLR to the 11E bridge on the Watauga arm) could be misread as a lake trout fishery; it is a harvest rule for tailwater-origin fish entering from upstream — TWRA's own narrative and survey reports contain no lake trout program.
2. GBIF's single 1954 rainbow record (36.3693,−82.298) is a 1950s agency data point; do not cite it as modern presence.
3. The drawdown-era reputation (2015–2022 "famous" bass fishing) is real but the dated sources frame it as: drawdown 2014–2021, refill March–July 2021, then an F1 trophy program to capitalize on reflooded vegetation — the trophy payoff is prospective in the sources (S5: "we are hopeful… trophy largemouth"), not yet a documented state record.
