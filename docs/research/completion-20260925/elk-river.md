### S1. Management Plan for the Tims Ford Tailwater Trout Fishery (2017–2022) — KEY SOURCE
- **Title/Author/Org:** "Management Plan for the Tims Ford Tailwater Trout Fishery (2017–2022)", James C. Pipas & Justin G. Spaulding, TWRA Region 2; drafted Nov 2016, approved Apr 2017 (Bobby Wilson, Asst Director; Frank Fiss, Chief of Fisheries; Todd St. John, Region 2 Fisheries Manager).
- **Publication date:** April 2017. **Observation window covered:** 1970–2016 sampling/stocking history; plan horizon 2017–2022.
- **Retrieval:** 2026-09-25 via Wayback (archived copy of https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/twra/documents/Tims-Ford-Tailwater-Management-Plan_2017-2022.pdf)
- **URL:** https://web.archive.org/web/2018/https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/twra/documents/Tims-Ford-Tailwater-Management-Plan_2017-2022.pdf (live tn.gov URL returns AEM error page to plain fetch)
- **Type:** Agency management plan (planned + retrospective). **Confidence:** HIGH (signed TWRA plan).
- **Establishes:**
  - Stocking months, explicit: "20,000 are Brown Trout (stocked **in April and May** of each year) and 38,000 are Rainbow Trout (stocked **March through December** of each year)"; Brook Trout 3,000/yr requested since 2012 (initial 2012 stocking 11,119; 22,159 brooks 2012–2016).
  - **December stocking documented:** "only one trout stocking (**December**) occurs at Old Dam Ford" (Management Recommendations).
  - **Completed surplus stockings in Nov + Dec:** "In November of 2008, surplus Rainbow Trout (4,906) from Flintville hatchery were stocked in the Elk River tailwater. Additionally, 1,501 Rainbow Trout were stocked in the Elk River tailwater in **December of 2014**."
  - **Holdover (multi-facet):** "Since 2008, holdover Brown Trout have been increasing in abundance and holdover Rainbow Trout have remained uncommon"; 1987 aerator retrofit: "only a few holdover Brown Trout were detected in annual samples"; post-1999 stocking reduction: "yearly monitoring detected an increase in overwintering of both Brown and Rainbow trout (Cleveland et al. 1999, 2000)"; trophy-brown objective depends on "above average relative weights of 'holdover' Brown Trout (>10.0 inches)".
  - **Reproduction:** "Natural trout reproduction has not been documented, and the Tims Ford tailwater is maintained entirely through stocking." (Matches MTFF claim.)
  - Temperature/operations history: hypolimnial release cools ~40 river-miles; 1993 min-flow turbine + blowers + O2 injection (DO 6.0 mg/L); 2004 flood damaged min-flow turbine; 2005 forebay aeration; **2008 TVA release-regime change (sluicing/spilling/combined ops, Boulder Darter & Cracking Pearlymussel)** — "length of river suitable for year-round trout now ends at **Beans Creek (river mile 119)**"; fishery upgraded from put-and-take to "put-grow-and-take".
  - Annual electrofishing: standardized 2005; six sites, daytime, **second week of March** annually (earlier era: night sampling Feb/March). March CPUE tables 2008–2016 (browns 16.7–59.7 fish/hr total; ≥20" browns 0.7–3.0 fish/hr) — March samples by construction document fish that survived Dec–Feb.
  - Historical stocking numbers: catchable RBT 12,558–52,559/yr 1990–2005 (earliest records 1990); fingerling RBT 30,000–51,099/yr (ceased 2005); catchable BNT 10,002–38,939/yr. First trout stocked 1970 (rainbow + brown).
  - Creel surveys: 1986–88, 1995 (14,340 h), 2000 (7,858 h), 2013 (21,444 h), all Apr–Oct; catch rates 0.9–2.64 fish/hr ("good"–"excellent").
- **Does not establish:** any Jan–Feb stocking event; brook holdover beyond 2014–2015 detections.

### S2. TWRA Fisheries Report 01-42 — Elk River Creel Survey Results, April–October 2000
- **Author/Org:** Phillip W. Bettoli, Tennessee Cooperative Fishery Research Unit (TTU/USGS), for TWRA. **Published:** August 2001 (cover letter Aug 7, 2001 to Frank Fiss).
- **Retrieval:** 2026-09-25, Wayback capture 20100530133450.
- **URL:** https://web.archive.org/web/20100530133450/http://tn.gov/twra/fish/StreamRiver/tailtrout/TimsFordTW2000CreelSurvey.pdf
- **Type:** Agency-sponsored creel study. **Confidence:** HIGH.
- **Establishes (2000 stocking season, month-by-month):** monthly catchable rainbow stockings **April–November** (31,000); **8,100 catchable brown trout stocked in March**; fingerling RBT 21 June 2000 (15,000) and 5 Sept 2000 (30,000).
- **Holdover:** "Some brown trout longer than 25 cm were creeled during the 2000 survey, and those fish were **holdovers from earlier stockings**"; "as many as half of all harvested brown trout originated from stockings that took place before 2000"; **"Annual monitoring by TWRA has revealed that some rainbow trout and brown trout overwinter each year (Cleveland et al. 1999, 2000), particularly brown trout... brown trout longer than 400 mm total length were observed, indicating that some brown trout were carrying-over for several years."** Also: 1995 rainbows grew 10–13 mm/month but "few survived longer than 100 days post-stocking."
- **Temperature regime (observed):** dam-site and Old Dam Ford temps never >17 °C Apr–Jun; **20–23 °C July–September** (above optimal growth; not acutely lethal). 2001 thermistor logging planned along the tailwater.
- **Geography/coordinates:** Access map (Fig. 1): Dam/HWY 50, Farris Creek Bridge, Old Dam Ford (~0–9+ mi below dam); Old Dam Ford stocked summer+fall in 2000 and recommended eliminated due to warm temps/low use.
- **Does not establish:** winter stocking (survey ended October); winter fishing effort.

### S3. TVA "Running Hot and Cold" (Tims Ford Dam story)
- **Author/Org:** Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA.com, "Built for the People" series). **Publication date:** undated on page; Wayback capture 2020-08-14 (article predates capture; references post-2008 ops).
- **Retrieval:** 2026-09-25 via Wayback (live tva.com returned 403 to fetcher).
- **URL:** https://web.archive.org/web/20200814202629/https://www.tva.com/about-tva/our-history/built-for-the-people/running-hot-and-cold
- **Type:** Federal agency explainer. **Confidence:** HIGH for temperature regime.
- **Establishes:**
  - "**Tims Ford Dam is operated in such a way as to provide tailwater temperatures cold enough to support a thriving trout fishery**, yet warm enough to support the endangered boulder darter" downstream.
  - Trout fishery "maintained during the summer months by the water released through Tims Ford Dam. When TVA releases water from the dam, it is **very cold due to the depth of the reservoir**."
  - TVA targets temps "at the **Farris Creek stream gage (Elk River Mile 124)** within the targeted temperature range **for trout**" while keeping Fayetteville gage (ERM 93) seasonal (mid-80s °F late summer).
  - "**This area supports trout during the cooler fall and winter months**; however, historically, water temperatures between Old Dam Ford (ERM 120) and Fayetteville increase above trout preference during summer... conditions suitable for trout survival and growth are typically restricted to areas upstream of Old Dam Ford beginning in July. **TWRA identifies only the area from Old Dam Ford upstream to Tims Ford Dam as a trout tailwater** in its Stocked Trout Program."
  - TDEC-designated trout stream: Tims Ford Dam → Fayetteville (ERM 93). Aeration: two turbine blowers + forebay O2 injection; sluiceway min-flows; Tims Ford = first TVA hydro retrofitted with small min-flow generating unit.
  - Dam facts: ERM 133.3, Franklin County; closed Dec 1, 1970; single 36-MW unit.
- **Does not establish:** numeric release temperature; stocking months.

## 4. TVA release temperature regime (Q3 answer)
- Deep/hypolimnial withdrawal; "very cold" release water (S3); TDEC trout-stream designation dam→Fayetteville ERM 93.
- Operations target: keep Farris Creek gage (ERM 124) within **trout** temperature range year-round; allow seasonal warming (mid-80s °F) at Fayetteville (ERM 93) for the boulder darter. Trout-suitable water in summer confined upstream of ~Old Dam Ford/Beans Creek (ERM 120/119); fall–winter suitable water extends toward Fayetteville (S3, S1).
- Historical fix timeline: 1993 min-flow + blowers + O2 injection (DO ≥6.0 mg/L) → 2004 flood damage → 2005 forebay aeration (RRI) → **2008 release-regime change** (sluice/spill/combine; mimic natural flows) → modern "put-grow-and-take" fishery; pre-2005 summer DO/temperature "prevented most trout from holding over" (S4), while some overwintering of browns persisted even in the 1990s (S1 citing Cleveland et al.).
- Observed in-river (2000, S2): ≤17 °C Apr–Jun; 20–23 °C Jul–Sep at dam and Old Dam Ford — the pre-2008 baseline the RRI program improved on.

## 5. Holdover / reproduction verdict (Q2 answer)
- **Holdover: DOCUMENTED, multi-line, multi-facet.** (a) TWRA annual March electrofishing (S1, S4) — brown CPUE 16.7–59.7/hr (2008–2016) with ≥20" browns in most years; March sampling inherently samples fish that survived Dec–Feb; (b) creel-documented holdover browns incl. multi-year carryover >400 mm (S2); (c) "holdover Brown Trout increasing in abundance since 2008" (S1); (d) TWRA StoryMap "trout can be found 24-7-365 in the first 11 miles" (S4); (e) MTFF "plenty of holdovers" (S11); (f) brook holdover detected 2014–2015 only (S1); (g) rainbow holdover is the weak link — "remained uncommon" (S1), "little evidence of significant multi-year holdover" (S4) — but rainbows persist across winters as a cohort ("fall-winter stockings" seen in March 2026 samples).
- **Reproduction: NONE.** "Natural trout reproduction has not been documented, and the Tims Ford tailwater is maintained entirely through stocking" (S1); MTFF concurs (S11). MTFF statement matches agency position — no contradiction.

## 6. Completed releases across seasons (Q4 answer)
- Documented destination-level completions ever found: **Nov 2008** (4,906 surplus RBT, S1), **7 Nov 2018** (S9), **Dec 2014** (1,501 RBT, S1) + annual **December** Old Dam Ford stocking (S1 plan; ArcGIS "Winter" program site, S8), **16 May 2024** (live feed), **10 Sep 2026** (live feed). Every season except Dec–Feb has ≥1 completion; **December has direct completions (2014 + recurring Old Dam Ford)**.
- **No January or February completion ever found** in any archived or live feed. The Winter Trout Program (S10) does not include the tailwater; 2026 JSON has no Jan–Feb row (S7). January–February presence is therefore covered by holdover evidence + thermal regime, not by stocking records.

## 7. Contradictions & reconciliations
1. **Mar–Nov (S5, 2010–2015) vs Mar–Dec (S1 2016/17, S6 2020, S7/S8 2026).** Real program change; the December addition is operationalized at Old Dam Ford (December-only stocking, S1). Ledger's Mar–Dec matches the modern era; pre-2016 schedules support one month less.
2. **Species: rainbow+brown (S5) vs +brook (S1 2012 onward, S6, S8).** Brook row is post-2012 accurate; old pages simply predate it. Ledger lists all three (correct for current era).
3. **"Year-round suitable habitat" (S4, first 11 mi) vs "year-round trout now ends at Beans Creek RM 119" (S1) vs Old Dam Ford "trout only in the winter" (S4).** Consistent once keyed to river mile: RM 133 dam → RM 120 Old Dam Ford ≈ 13.3 mi; Beans Creek RM 119 just below Old Dam Ford; StoryMap's "11 miles" is the conservative angling core. The ledger line ending at Old Dam Ford aligns with the cold reach.
