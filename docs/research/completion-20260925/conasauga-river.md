# CONASAUGA RIVER — TENNESSEE REACH (southeast Polk County; Cherokee NF; flows to GA/Coosa basin) — warmwater evidence log

Ledger water: `warmwater-focus`. Identity discipline: this is the Conasauga RIVER (Polk Co. TN headwaters → GA → Oostanaula → Coosa → Gulf), a State Scenic River; NOT "Conasauga CREEK" (Monroe Co., Tellico drainage, which IS a TWRA trout water with delayed harvest); NOT the GA Cohutta reaches. All retrievals: 2026-09-25. Research only.

## A. Verdict summary (recommendation)

- **Classification: WARMWATER (confirmed). Confidence: HIGH** (for the documented absence of any TWRA trout program; the reach is a nationally famous native warmwater/coolwater fish-diversity river, not a trout fishery).
- The TN reach: 8 miles in Tennessee (TN SWAP COA) / 10.1 designated scenic miles in Polk County (TDEC GIS). "More than 90 species of fish" (2025 COA; 70+ in 2015 COA); some pools with 40+ species. Signature rare fauna: Conasauga logperch, snail darter/amber darter, regionally famous USFS snorkeling reach.
- **Zero trout stocking: no Conasauga row/site in any TWRA trout dataset 2003–2026**; not in the 2026 e-regulations trout regs; the only "Conasauga" in live TWRA regs pages is a CRAYFISH live-bait list, not a trout list. GBIF/iNat salmonid queries in the TN-reach bbox: 0.

## B. Identity / documentation of the TN reach (dated sources)

1. **Tennessee State Wildlife Action Plan — Conasauga COA, 2025 edition** (TWRA; TN_2025_COA ArcGIS layer, OBJECTID with COA "Conasauga", Region 3, document attachment 51; PDF cached `conasauga_coa_2025.pdf/txt`, retrieved 2026-09-25): "The river flows through **Tennessee for eight miles** before it turns south back into Georgia; it is the **only river that flows through Tennessee that does not drain into the Mississippi River** [i.e., drains to the Gulf/Coosa]... providing a home to **more than 90 species of fish**. Some pools within the river have inventory lists of greater than 40 fish species... Public lands within the COA: South Cherokee National Forest, Conasauga State Scenic River." 2. **Same COA, 2015 edition** (TN SWAP COA PDF, archived 2021-08-23, cached `conasauga_coa_2015.pdf/txt`): identical course text; "more than 70 species of fish"; water quality "excellent within the national forests." Establishes a dated 2015→2025 agency documentation chain.

2. TN-reach length differs by source: "eight miles" (COA text, both editions) vs 10.1 GIS miles (TDEC scenic layer, upstream of Hwy 411 bridge) — different segment definitions; both agency sources.
