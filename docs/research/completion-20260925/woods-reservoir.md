# Woods Reservoir (Franklin County — AEDC) — warmwater confirmation log

- Log date / retrieval date: 2026-09-25
- Classification target: warmwater-focus reservoir (AEDC/Elk River impoundment near Manchester, Franklin County). Prior passes: TWRA warmwater (top-3 crappie, walleye, PCB advisory; INRMP 2006); Bradley Creek agent documented "no trout program anywhere in the complex". This pass confirms/strengthens with dated sources and a zero-trout negative check.

## 1. Warmwater evidence (dated sources)

**Source 1 — TWRA "Woods Reservoir in Tennessee" fishing page** (tn.gov, https://www.tn.gov/twra/fishing/where-to-fish/middle-tennessee-r2/woods-reservoir.html; retrieved 2026-09-25, fetched twice; no on-page date):
- "Woods Reservoir is a 3,660-acre impoundment located in south-central Tennessee with a major portion in Franklin County." "The US Air Force and Arnold Engineering Development Center own and operate Woods Reservoir." "Dam completion occurred in 1952."
- "The best fishing opportunities are for Largemouth Bass, Crappie, White Bass, Yellow Bass, and Channel Catfish."
- "Crappie fishing is the second most popular fishery and accounts for 30 percent of the targeted angler effort." "Both White and Black Crappie are abundant in Woods Reservoir."
- Consumption advisory: "an advisory not to eat catfish from Woods Reservoir due to PCB contamination."
- TWRA fish attractors (stake beds) "most productive for crappie from October through May in 6-12 feet of water."
- The word "trout" appears only in the site navigation menu, never in page content — zero trout program, zero trout stockings on the official water page.

**Source 2 — Arnold Air Force Base, "AEDC at 75: Groundwork begins"** (arnold.af.mil; dated Mar 23, 2026 in search result; retrieved via search 2026-09-25): the Elk River Dam project "was completed in September 1952. The construction of the Elk River Dam created Woods Reservoir," built to supply cooling water for AEDC's test facilities (~26 billion gallons, ~3,980 acres per the same coverage).

**Source 3 — Mississippi Sportsman (mshfn.com), "Tennessee's Big-City Crappie Hotspots", April 10, 2020** (surfaced via search 2026-09-25): TWRA biologists rate Woods Reservoir one of the top three crappie lakes in Tennessee based on per-acre production. (Secondary, dated; consistent with the prior INRMP-2006 top-3 note and with the TWRA page's 30%-of-effort crappie statistic.)

**Supporting:** TN.gov "Southern Middle TN Reservoir Bass Information" (surfaced 2026-09-25): Woods is the shallowest of the three southern Middle Tennessee reservoirs, managed for bass. Walleye: listed in the TWRA page's what-you-can-catch (regulations) — walleye present/managed, though a dated walleye-stocking record for Woods specifically did not surface (walleye-stocking news found only for other TWRA lakes, e.g., Carroll Lake 2022-2023).

## 2. Zero-trout note (negative check)

- Search "Woods Reservoir trout stocking Tennessee" (2026-09-25): no result stocks trout in Woods; nearest stocked trout water surfaced is Boiling Fork Creek (Region 3). Trout stocking in Tennessee targets tailwaters/cold streams, not this warm impoundment.
- TWRA trout datasets cross-check (local archive, inspected 2026-09-25): "Woods" does not appear in the 2026 schedule JSON (616 rows), the 2024 completed feed (54 rows), the 2022 completed feeds, the 2003-2015/2018-2025 schedule PDFs, the winter-program lists (2014/15, 2017/18), or the ArcGIS trout-stocking-locations layer (730 features).
- Consistent with the Bradley Creek agent's finding: no trout program anywhere in the Woods complex.
