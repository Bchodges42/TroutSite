# Brimstone Creek (Scott County, TN — New River tributary) — evidence log

Research date: 2026-09-24. Research only; no agency/author contact. All retrieval dates 2026-09-24.
Question: has any reach-specific survey/observation ever documented fish in Brimstone Creek, and is there any trout basis?

## Reach definition

- Scott County, Cumberland Plateau. Rises south of Elgin (~36.09 N, -84.53 W), flows N/NW roughly 14–15 mi through the Brimstone community to the New River confluence (New River HUC10 0513010403; HUC12 "Phillips Creek-New River" area). Lower reach at/near Slick Rock; drainage is the most heavily coal-mined subbasin of the Big South Fork cataloging unit.
- Anchor stations (WQP): TDECWPC-BRIMS014.3SC / TDECWR_WQX-TNW000000610 "Brimstone Creek" (36.2525, -84.5063); TDECWPC-BRIMS009.2SC / TNW000000609 (36.3042, -84.5058); USGS-03408200 "Brimstone Creek near Robbins" (36.3454, -84.5394, near mouth); USACOEND-3WOL10074 "Brimstone Creek Mile 3.1" (36.3453, -84.5392, co-located with gauge).
- 2007 museum sample at "Brimstone road crossing" at Slick Rock (lower reach, near the New River junction).

## (2) Fish surveys / species lists

**NPS Natural Resource Condition Assessment, Big South Fork (Worsham, Sundin, Nibbelink, Mengak, Grossman — UGA Warnell; NPS/BISO/NRR—2013/619, 2013)** — retrieval 2026-09-24; URL: https://npshistory.com/publications/biso/nrr-2013-619.pdf (10.6 MB PDF, text-extracted and grepped in full).
- Fish chapter 4.7 synthesizes all BISO-drainage fish studies: Cope 1870, Kirsch 1893, Shoup & Peyton 1940, Comiskey & Etnier 1972, Brazinski 1979, O'Bara et al. 1982, **Evans 1998**, Carter et al. 2005, **Scott 2010 (NPS I&M 2003–2006 dataset = Tables 33–35 basis)**. Appendix D = drainage-wide species × study matrix (1870–2010, 89 species incl. 4 non-natives); Tables 33/34 = BISO-wide abundance/occurrence; Table 35 = 14 reporting areas; Table 39 = New River reporting area.
- New River reporting area (0513010403): 20 samples in analysis dataset (3 in park), 3,844 individuals of 42 species, 4 species of concern, 2 non-native, 34% intolerant, mean SDI 0.67, mean KIBI 52 = "fair"; trend "improving"; data quality "good". O'Bara et al. (1982) collected ZERO samples in this reporting area.
- **"Brimstone" appears exactly once in ~260 pp** — as one of three subwatershed names in the New River water-quality section. No Brimstone-specific fish sample, site, or IBI anywhere in the report (Tables 33–35 and Appendix D carry no per-stream attribution).
- Salmonid content of the entire 89-species drainage list: ONE — brook trout (Salvelinus fontinalis), sole report by Shoup & Peyton 1940, "a single location following hatchery introductions" (drainage-wide, not Brimstone). Musky (Esox masquinongy) native, from 19th-century reports.
- Evans (1998) synthesis: "Distribution of fishes and changes in biotic integrity in the New River, Tennessee," M.S. thesis, Univ. of Tennessee, Knoxville, 168 pp; sampled 41 sites across the New River drainage (NRCA: 21 of 41 scored "poor"/"very poor"; 41 species reported; improvement vs Brazinski 1979). Site-level localities are NOT reproduced in the NRCA — whether any of the 41 sites was on Brimstone Creek is unresolved (thesis is print-only; not digitized).
- Type: broad method-documented survey synthesis → weighted NEGATIVE evidence for trout in the whole New River drainage (non-detection at drainage level; Brimstone-specific coverage not shown). Establishes: drainage-level warmwater assemblage (42 spp incl. darters/suckers/minnows), no trout; does NOT by itself establish Brimstone Creek occupancy.

**GBIF-vouchered fish collections with "Brimstone Creek" locality** — retrieval 2026-09-24; URLs: https://api.gbif.org/v1/occurrence/search?q=%22Brimstone%20Creek%22&taxonKey=1 and https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=%22Brimstone%20Creek%22 . THIS ANSWERS THE LEDGER QUESTION — yes, fish have been documented on Brimstone Creek, repeatedly:
1. **1891** — Percina caprodes (logperch), "Brimstone Creek New B., Tennessee," collector P. Kirsch, USNM (Smithsonian, dataset 821cc27a). = the Kirsch 1893 survey the NRCA cites ("seined six flows, 24 species" — Brimstone was one).
2. **1893** — Hypentelium nigricans (northern hog sucker), "Brimstone Creek, New River, Tennessee," United States Fish Commission, USNM.
3. **1953** — Academy of Natural Sciences Ichthyology Collection (dataset de22e531): "Brimstone Creek, 2 3/4 mi S of New River (Town)" — 4 records (Percina maculata; Percidae; Catostomidae; Ictaluridae).
4. **1968** — University of Alabama Ichthyology Collection (AUM; dataset e90a588b): Percina maculata, "Brimstone Creek, 3 mi E of Robbins (Big South Fork)," collectors R. Hitch & C. Comiskey.
5. **2007** — UF FLMNH lots 172952–172957 (dataset eccf4b09), collectors Lawrence Page, A. Thomson, Rebecca Blanton-Johansen, Scott Co.: Luxilus chrysocephalus (striped shiner), Campostoma oligolepis (largescale stoneroller), Etheostoma blennioides (greenside darter), **Etheostoma camurum (bluebreast darter — intolerant/clean-water)**, Etheostoma caeruleum (rainbow darter), Etheostoma spectabile (orangethroat darter). Locality: "Brimstone Creek and New River at Slick Rock at Brimstone road crossing" (site may straddle the lower creek near the confluence; sample is Brimstone-attributed).
- Method: museum voucher specimens (seine/electroshock-era collections; 2007 lots = 6 species). Type: specimen-based occurrence → ESTABLISHES fish occurrence in Brimstone Creek (5 dated episodes, 1891–2007). Species omitted: all Salmonidae — zero trout specimens ever.

## (4) Contradictions and caveats

- Prior-pass context said "Evans 1998, 42 New River localities"; the NRCA text says 41 sites / 41 species. Minor discrepancy; the thesis itself (print-only) is the arbiter and was not retrievable online (Bing, WorldCat, archive.org all failed).
- Prior context implied TWRA 2004/2010 New River chapters with tributary IBIs (Smoky Creek 21 spp, Beech Fork 18 spp). NRCA contains no Beech Fork mention at all and no Brimstone tributary IBI; those TWRA chapters were not locatable this session (see searches). Their Brimstone content remains unknown.
- The 2007 UF locality string ("Brimstone Creek and New River at Slick Rock") could straddle the confluence; the 1891/1893/1953/1968 localities are unambiguously Brimstone Creek, so the "documented fish" finding does not hinge on it.
- NRCA 2003–06 dataset (Scott 2010): no Brimstone-specific samples; "fair KIBI 52, improving" is reporting-area-level (HUC10), not creek-level.

## Recommendation

**Warmwater-focus** (high confidence). Reasons: (a) fish occurrence in Brimstone Creek is established by five dated vouchered collections 1891–2007, all warmwater/cleanwater species (incl. intolerant bluebreast darter in 2007), zero Salmonidae; (b) dated summer temperatures 22–26 °C at the lower gauge (1975–1983) and 16–22 °C mid-basin (2014–2019); (c) zero TWRA stocking anywhere on the water (schedule JSON + ArcGIS); (d) drainage-wide, the NRCA's 89-species list contains exactly one historical, hatchery-era brook trout record (1940, elsewhere in the drainage). Non-detection caveats apply to Trout-only inference but the classification question is settled: classify and manage as warmwater; do not carry a trout or seasonal-stocked expectation.
