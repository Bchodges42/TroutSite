# Clear Fork (Scott County, TN) — Trout Evidence Research Log

Water: Clear Fork, western parent fork of the Big South Fork of the Cumberland (drains the New River system's western/wild branch; North Prong + South Prong join near Burrville/Rugby; main stem runs NE past Brewster Bridge (SR 52) → Peters Bridge → Burnt Mill Bridge → mouth at New River confluence (~36.4217, -84.6247), forming the BSF just inside/at the BISO boundary). HUC 0513010402 (Clear Fork River).
Ledger state entering: unresolved; 2026 warm gauge reading at one point; no reach-specific trout survey known.
Retrieval date for all sources: 2026-09-24.
SAME-NAME RISKS handled (all explicitly disambiguated below): (a) KY "Clear Fork of the Cumberland" (Campbell Co TN → Whitley Co KY; USGS gage at Saxton, KY) — monitored by TWRA HCP; (b) OH Clear Fork of the Mohican (Harrison/Morrow Co OH — dominates "Clear Fork muskie" web content; Fishbrain "Clear Fork, Harrison County, Ohio"); (c) Clear Fork (Logan/Warren Co KY, C&R water); (d) Clearfork WV (Coal R.); (e) KY McCreary Co stream names near BISO (Rock Creek, Little South Fork) — BSF tributaries, not Clear Fork. Also within our basin: "White Oak Creek" (south, Sunbright area, TRIBUTARY OF CLEAR FORK) vs "North White Oak Creek" (separate direct BSF tributary — NOT Clear Fork).

## 1. Comiskey & Etnier 1970 — the keystone survey (primary, high confidence)

- Source: Comiskey, C.E. 1970. "The Fishes of the Big South Fork of the Cumberland River System." M.S. Thesis, University of Tennessee, Knoxville (major professor D.A. Etnier). Survey window June 28, 1968 – Sept 21, 1969 (summer low-flow seasons both years).
- URL: https://trace.tennessee.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/0f398cda-4e26-41a5-b602-522f33f3cb61/content (full PDF; OCR text extracted)
- Methods (thesis Ch. II): 10/20-ft small-mesh seines, 30/60-ft bag seines, gill nets, sodium cyanide; effort concentrated at road crossings; full species lists per collection in Appendix A with dates, collectors, counts.
- Clear Fork coverage: "Clear Fork River drainage was sampled seventeen times" (p. 5). MAIN-STEM stations (Appendix A):
  - U.T. BSF 25 — Clear Fork River at Brewster bridge on TN-52, Morgan–Fentress Co. line, June 28, 1968 (Bouchard, Smythe, Etnier, Comiskey): 15 spp incl. Etheostoma camurum (57), Nocomis micropogon (9), Percina squamata (4). NO salmonids.
  - U.T. BSF 24 — Clear Fork River at junction of Crooked Creek, 4 air mi S of Armawaithe, Morgan–Fentress line, July 15, 1969 (Hitch, Comiskey): 4 spp (partial list as printed; Labidesthes sicculus present). NO salmonids.
  - U.T. BSF 31 — Clear Fork River at Burnt Mill bridge, ~3¼ mi upstream of New River junction, Scott Co., July 24, 1968 (Hitch, Comiskey): 19 spp incl. Micropterus dolomieui (4), Ictalurus punctatus, Pylodictis olivaris (1), Percina copelandi (37), P. squamata (5). NO salmonids.
  - U.T. BSF 32 — Clear Fork River at Burnt Mill bridge, 3.5 air mi NW of Robbins, Scott Co., July 26, 1969 (Hitch, Comiskey): 9 spp. NO salmonids.
  - Tributary/headwater stations of the Clear Fork drainage: BSF 16 (N Prong 1 mi E of Grimsley, 7/16/1969), BSF 17 (N Prong at Co. Rd 4450, 3 mi NNE Roslin?, 7/26/1969), BSF 18 (S Prong 2 mi NNE Roslin, 7/26/1969), BSF 19 (Clear Fork at Gatewood bridge, 7/26/1969), BSF 22 (Crooked Creek 100 yd above Clear Fork mouth, 7/15/1969), BSF 23 (Big Creek trib, 7/26/1969), plus Whiteoak Creek stations BSF 26 (7/15/1969), BSF 27 (9/13/1968) — southern White Oak Cr., a Clear Fork tributary.
- Trout findings (species account, Family Salmonidae, p. 12-13): "Salmo trutta Linnaeus—Brown trout. Found at only one locality in the system, the specimen was undoubtedly stocked in the stream by fish and game personnel." That locality = U.T. BSF 37, Laurel Fork of NORTH WHITEOAK Creek, Fentress Co., Sept 6, 1968 (1 specimen) — Appendix B attributes Salmo trutta to the "North Whiteoak Creek Drainage" list, NOT the Clear Fork drainage. NOTE REACH DISCIPLINE: N Whiteoak Cr. is an adjacent direct BSF tributary, outside HUC 0513010402.
- Brook trout/rainbow: "Shoup and Peyton (1940) reported Salvelinus fontinalis and Salmo gairdneri from Pickett Lake, a part of the Rock Creek drainage. These specimens were also the result of stocking, and are not known to occur naturally anywhere in the system. Rock Creek in Kentucky may now have a reproducing trout population, since much stocking has occurred..." (Rock Creek = BSF tributary with TN headwaters in Pickett Co; KY-side fishery, not Clear Fork.)
- Appendix B "II. CLEAR FORK DRAINAGE": 29 species (Ambloplites rupestris … Semotilus atromaculatus, incl. Pylodictis olivaris, Percina copelandi, P. squamata, Labidesthes sicculus, Ictalurus natalis). ZERO salmonids.
- What it establishes: strong, method-documented, season-limited (summer) negative evidence for trout in the TN Clear Fork main stem at 4 road-crossing stations + 7 drainage stations in 1968–69, full community lists, no salmonids anywhere in the drainage. Does not establish: current absence, winter/early-spring conditions, or conditions in the unroaded gorge between crossings.
- Thesis notes oil pollution in Whiteoak Creek portion of the Clear Fork drainage (Shoup 1940) reported cleaned up by 1968-69; New River drainage still polluted with acid/strip-mine effects during the study.

## 2. NPS Natural Resource Condition Assessment, BISO (NRR-2013/619) — modern in-park fish data (primary synthesis, high confidence)

- Source: Worsham, Sundin, Nibbelink, Mengak, Grossman (Univ. of Georgia) for NPS, January 2013. URL: https://npshistory.com/publications/biso/nrr-2013-619.pdf
- Underlying fish data: Scott, E.M. 2010. Fish survey of Big South Fork National River and Recreation Area. TVA/NPS Cooperative Agreement FS5000-02-0795 (cited in NRCA bibliography, p. ~148). Samples Oct 2003 – Nov 2006; 90 samples; 16,820 individuals; 78 species; methods: backpack electroshocker, boat electroshocker, seines, gillnets, dipnets, snorkeling, minnow traps.
- Clear Fork reporting area (HUC 0513010402), NRCA text + Table 35 (p. 129): analysis dataset contains 4 samples at 2 locations ON THE CLEAR FORK RIVER WITHIN BISO boundaries; 990 individuals; 23 species; 32% intolerant individuals; 1 species of concern; 0 NON-NATIVE species; mean SDI 0.76; mean KIBI 45 ("fair"). Samples collected by boat (2 not usable for KIBI). Sample density 2nd-lowest of 14 reporting areas; condition NOT ranked (Table 38, p. 131); no trend (no historical baseline — O'Bara et al. 1982 did not sample this area).
- What it establishes: modern (2003–06) main-stem community samples exist and contain zero non-native species — trout (all three species non-native here) absent from 990 individuals across 2 main-stem locations, multiple seasons across 3 years. Per-site species lists are NOT printed in the NRCA (only top-10 tables 33/34, drainage metrics 35/36); the full 23-species list lives in the Scott 2010 dataset (see "Key gap").
- Salmonid synthesis (NRCA §4.7, p. ~122 and Appendix D, PDF pp. 283–286): "Shoup and Peyton (1940) provided the sole report of brook trout (Salvelinus fontinalis) which occurred at a single location following hatchery introductions." Appendix D matrix (studies 1870–2010: NPSpecies, APHN, Scott 2010, Evans 1998, O'Bara 1982, Brazinski 1979, Comiskey & Etnier, Shoup & Peyton 1940, Kirsch 1893, Cope 1870):
  - Salvelinus fontinalis: ONLY Shoup & Peyton 1940 (Pickett Lake, hatchery).
  - Salmo trutta*: Evans 1998 and Comiskey & Etnier columns only (both single stocked fish, neither on Clear Fork main stem).
  - Oncorhynchus mykiss*: NPSpecies Dbase and Shoup & Peyton 1940 columns only.
- Water-quality context for Clear Fork (NRCA §3/§4): Rikard et al. (1986) classified Clear Fork "slightly polluted" (elevated sulfate/conductivity at 4 stations, increasing downstream into BISO, attributed partly to White Oak Cr.); Johnson (2003) found elevated Fe/Mn in tributaries 1975–81, Cu/Pb on Clear Fork through the 90s; 2004–06 data sparse (1 station near New River junction; low alkalinity; pH ~7.5); >1,400 oil/gas wells in the cataloging unit mostly in Clear Fork and N White Oak drainages (Guyot 2005). HUC 0513010402 water-quality status "fair, no trend."
- NPS WQP stations confirm the reach mapped in this HUC (from WQP Station query): "Clear Fork at Peters Bridge" (36.3250, -84.7866), "Clear Fork 100 M Upstream of Peters Bridge" (36.3257, -84.7858), "Clear Fork at Brewster Bridge" (36.3518, -84.7278), "Clear Fork at USGS Gage at Burnt Mill Bridge" (36.3882, -84.6301), "Clear Fork at Mouth" (36.4217, -84.6247).

## 3. Evans 1998 — New River system proper (primary, high confidence; reach check DONE)

- Source: Evans, R. Brian. 1998. "Distribution of Fishes and Changes in Biotic Integrity in the New River, Tennessee." M.S. Thesis, University of Tennessee, Knoxville, 169 pp.
- URL (full PDF): https://trace.tennessee.edu/utk_gradthes/2585/ (bitstream: https://trace.tennessee.edu/bitstreams/999ff059-a45d-459e-b031-dbc0dd2b0517/download)
- Methods/coverage: 42 localities in 25 second- to sixth-order streams, "in the New River basin," May–October 1996, plus one large-river site (site 42, New River RM 4.6+) April 1997; backpack electrofishing (150 V AC), seine supplements; representatives archived UT fish collection.
- Reach check: NO Clear Fork localities — all stations are New River proper and its tributaries (Buffalo Cr., Brimstone Cr., Straight Fork, Paint Rock Cr., Phillips Cr., New River main stem, etc.). Clear Fork references are secondhand (other studies). NO SALMONIDS anywhere in the species accounts or Appendix 2 lists.
- Clear Fork main-stem fish records it cites (historical, 1970s–80s):
  - Walleye: "Riddle (1975) captured five individuals from Clear Fork" (muskellunge survey 1973–75). Also Comiskey & Etnier gillnetted walleye in the BSF main stem.
  - Flathead catfish: "collected throughout the Big South Fork and in lower Clear Fork."
  - Redear sunfish: single specimen "from Clear Fork in 1981 (UT 90.593)" — introduced-fish interpretation (farm pond/bait bucket/Lake Cumberland recruit).
  - Striped shiner: Comiskey found it at 2 sites on White Oak Creek (southern, Clear Fork tributary); Evans interprets bait-bucket introduction.
- Riddle citation: Riddle, J.W. 1975. Status of the native muskellunge, Esox masquinongy ohioensis, of the Cumberland Plateau, Tennessee. M.S. Thesis, Tennessee Technological University, 70 p. (NOT retrieved — see Key gap.)
- What it establishes: the most recent whole-watershed-style survey (1996) again found no trout, but covered the NEW RIVER basin, not Clear Fork — its value for our reach is indirect (no trout in the sister fork; historical cool/warm-water gamefish records FOR Clear Fork: walleye, flathead).

## 6. USGS gage 03409500 "Clear Fork near Robbins" (physical habitat evidence, primary data)

- URL: https://waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/iv/?format=json&sites=03409500&startDT=2026-07-01&endDT=2026-08-31&parameterCd=00010 (gage site = Burnt Mill Bridge reach; NPS co-located station "Clear Fork at USGS Gage at Burnt Mill Bridge" 36.3882, -84.6301)

- Summer 2025 (same period, n=2972): min 21.0 °C, max 30.6 °C, mean 26.2 °C.

Even the SUMMER MINIMUM (~21 °C) exceeds brown/rainbow growth-optimum; maxima are at acute tolerance limits. This is decisive against summer trout persistence at this reach (consistent with the ledger's "2026 warm gauge reading"; here shown repeatable in 2025). 2023/2024 pulls failed (HTTP 301/rate limit) — gap, not contradiction.
- Does NOT cover: headwater prongs (USGS 03408550 "North Prong Clear Fork near Grimsley" is a listed station but returned no IV temp series in query window); gorge micro-refugia.

- GMP 2005 "brown and rainbow trout have been stocked in three streams by state agencies" (unnamed) vs. TWRA schedule/site layers with no BSF-drainage stream sites — most consistent with KY-side waters (Rock Creek KY etc.); unresolved lead, not evidence for Clear Fork.

## RECOMMENDATION: warmwater-focus (with a narrow, explicitly-labeled cold-headwater caveat)

Reasoning:
1. Depth of negative survey evidence: the only two method-documented community surveys that touched the TN Clear Fork main stem — 1968–69 (4 main-stem + 7 drainage stations, seines/gillnets/cyanide, full lists) and 2003–06 (2 in-park main-stem locations, 990 individuals, 0 non-native) — both omitted trout entirely. NRCA's 1870–2010 Appendix D synthesis confirms no trout record has EVER been attributed to the Clear Fork drainage by any study.
2. No stocking infrastructure: absent from TWRA's 2026 schedule (616-row machine-readable), absent from the 730-site stocking map, absent from tailwater program; the only regional salmonid stockings are KY-side (Rock Creek) and pond/winter programs (Oneida City Park Lake).
3. Thermal reality: USGS 03409500 at Burnt Mill Bridge shows summer means of 25.7–26.2 °C and maxima of 30.2–30.6 °C in consecutive recent summers (2025, 2026) — summer salmonid persistence in the sampled reach is physically implausible, making any seasonal-stocked scenario unworkable at that reach absent cold-water refugia.
4. Community record is uniformly and repeatedly warmwater (smallmouth/rock bass/longear; TDEC tissue program targets smallmouth three times 2011–2019) with no single trout catch report found even as a lead (the only "trout" claims are an auto-generated app list and regional KY/BSF-confused content).
Why not "unresolved": the remaining unknowns (remote gorge, headwater prongs) are exactly the reaches where no data exist, but the burden of evidence — zero salmonid records in 60 years, zero stocking, lethal summer temps at the only instrumented point — now points one direction. Why not "seasonal-stocked": no evidence any stocking ever occurred there.
Caveat to carry: the North/South Prong headwaters (Grimsley/Roslin/Gatewood area) and the roadless gorge have no temperature or survey data post-1970; Comiskey's July 1969 prong stations found no salmonids, but cool-headwater refuge cannot be fully excluded from desk evidence alone.

## Key gap + likely record holder

Gap: no modern (post-2006) reach-specific fish survey or temperature record for the Clear Fork gorge or headwater prongs; the full 23-species list for the two 2003–06 in-park main-stem stations was never published.
Likely record holder: NPS Big South Fork NRRA / TVA — the Scott (2010) fish survey dataset (TVA/NPS Cooperative Agreement FS5000-02-0795; raw data in the NPS APHN fish database / BISO park files; Scott also holds tuxedo darter monitoring 1993–2005). Secondary: Tennessee Tech (Riddle 1975 thesis) for the 1973–75 muskie/walleye Clear Fork records; TDEC (fish tissue program) for ongoing smallmouth sampling at Burnt Mill reach.
