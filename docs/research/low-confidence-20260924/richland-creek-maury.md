# Richland Creek (Giles/Maury County, TN; Elk River tributary) — Trout-Stocking Evidence Research Log

Water: Richland Creek, Giles County TN (main stem, Pulaski to Elk River confluence near Elkton) with headwaters reaching toward the Maury County line; distinguished by county qualifier from other TN Richland Creeks (Rutherford, Davidson, Hawkins etc. — none share this HUC 06030004).
Research date: 2026-09-24 (all retrievals 2026-09-24).
Ledger at start: unresolved — community reports describe small-stream bass/panfish; nothing documented; STRONG LEAD to verify: Spaulding & Rogers 2020 (J. SE Assoc. Fish & Wildlife Agencies 7:33–40) TWRA boat-electrofishing smallmouth study covering "Elk River/Richland Creek," May–June 2018.
Researcher constraint: research only; no agency/business contact; no writes except this file.

## HEADLINE FINDING

The strong lead VERIFIES and is far stronger than a lead: Spaulding & Rogers 2020 is a TWRA/USGS-Coop-Unit boat-electrofishing study of exactly this creek. "Richland Creek (mean annual discharge = 15.8 m³/s) is the largest tributary to Elk River"; sampled over a 57-km reach at 11 sites with boat pulsed-DC electrofishing, May–June 2018. It documents a healthy smallmouth bass population (82 fish, 166–492 mm TL; CPUE 15 fish/h; PSD-P 23; ages 1–11; fish > 430 mm present) — a direct, method-documented agency fish survey of this Richland Creek with NO trout content (the paper's only trout reference is the Tims Ford tailwater on the Elk main stem, a different water 30+ km from the study's Richland reach).

That survey is now one of FOUR independent fish-evidence lanes, all warmwater, all trout-free:
- TWRA boat EF 2018 (82 smallmouth) — targeted single-species reporting, not a full species list;
- EPA National Rivers and Streams Assessment, Richland Creek site FW08TN024 (lower creek near the mouth), 2008-09-27: 39 fish taxa counted (~1,200 individuals), zero trout;
- University of Alabama Ichthyology Collection: 62 voucher records ON Richland Creek proper (1988–2007, four highway crossings), 35 species, zero trout;
- TDEC ambient temperature record along the creek: summer grab maxima 22–28.6 °C (lower station 28.56), matching the paper's "26–29 °C June–Sept" — a hard thermal exclusion of year-round trout.

Stocking lanes are all negative: no Richland row in the 2026 schedule (616 rows), zero Richland sites in TWRA's statewide ArcGIS trout layer (LIKE sweep), and no Richland row in any located historical schedule (2003, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2022). Community layer (Fishbrain/FishAngler/tndeer): smallmouth/largemouth/green sunfish, zero trout.

Bottom line: this is a well-documented warmwater smallmouth stream. Recommendation: warmwater-focus.

## SOURCE-BY-SOURCE LOG

### A. Agency survey evidence (the verified lead)

A1. Spaulding, J. and M. Rogers. 2020. "Smallmouth Bass Population Characteristics and Minimum Length Limit Evaluation in Two Tennessee Rivers." Journal of the Southeastern Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies 7:33–40.
- Orgs: TWRA (Spaulding, Nashville) and USGS Tennessee Cooperative Fishery Research Unit / Tennessee Tech (Rogers). Published 2020-03-01. Retrieved 2026-09-24 (landing page + full PDF).
- URLs: https://seafwa.org/journal/2020/smallmouth-bass-population-characteristics-and-minimum-length-limit-evaluation-two — PDF at https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/J7_04_Spaulding%252520and%252520Rogers%25252033-40.pdf (the href's double-encoded form is what actually serves the 1.2 MB file; the single-encoded form 404s; plain curl needs the double-encoded URL verbatim).
- Study design: Elk River sampled over a 117-km reach at 12 sites; Richland Creek over a 57-km reach at 11 sites; boat-mounted pulsed-DC electrofishing (Smith-Root GPP Type VI-A), 1800-sec transects, May–June 2018. All smallmouth measured/weighed; otoliths aged.
- Richland Creek results: 82 smallmouth, 166–492 mm TL; mean CPUE 15 fish/h (SE 6.2); PSD-P 23, PSD-M 10 (higher size structure than Elk); mean length and RSD-P higher than Elk; age range 1–11; annual mortality ≈0.45–0.46; fish enter the 254-mm fishery at 2.6 yr. Conclusion: no new MLL needed to prevent overfishing.
- Trout content: only contextual — "headwaters of Elk River are impounded by Tims Ford Dam, which created an 18-km tailwater capable of sustaining a year-round trout fishery (TVA 2008)... typically overlap about 30 km of the upstream Elk River sampling reach." Richland Creek itself: NO trout mention. Thermal note in paper: "From June through September, daily average water temperature for the lower Elk River and Richland Creek fluctuate between 26–29 °C (USGS water data gage 03584600 and 03604000)."
- CONTRADICTION/GAP found: cited gage 03604000 is actually BUFFALO RIVER NEAR FLAT WOODS, TN (NWIS lookup); the Richland Creek gage is 03584000 (Richland Creek near Pulaski) — almost certainly a typo in the paper. Does not change the temperature conclusion (TDEC's own record below confirms 26–29 °C class summer water).
- WHICH Richland Creek? The paper names no county. Identification as the Giles/Maury creek is supported by: (a) it is "the largest tributary to Elk River" with a 57-km boat-accessible reach — the Giles County Richland (Pulaski→Elkton) is the only such reach in the TN Elk basin (the paper's Richland mean annual discharge 15.8 m³/s ≈ 558 cfs matches LOWER Richland near its mouth; the near-Pulaski gage drainage gives roughly half that); (b) the Elk sampling reach overlaps the Tims Ford tailwater within 30 km of its upstream end and runs 117 km — i.e., Franklin/Marshall/Lincoln/Giles counties TN, ending near the Richland confluence; (c) independent corroboration below (museum + NARS + TDEC + community) that the Giles County Richland is exactly the smallmouth river the paper describes; (d) TWRA (Nashville/Region 2-3) manages the TN Richland. Confidence in the identification: HIGH.
- Type: peer-reviewed agency survey. Confidence: HIGH. Establishes: method-documented TWRA sampling of THIS Richland Creek in 2018 with a robust smallmouth population and no trout. Limitation per the standards: single-species reporting (other species captured were not listed), so it is not by itself a "full species list omitting trout" negative — but A2–A3 below supply exactly that.

## RECOMMENDATION

**Warmwater-focus.**
Reasoning:
- Direct agency survey exists and documents the creek as a smallmouth river: TWRA's own 2020 peer-reviewed study (11 boat-EF sites over 57 km, May–June 2018, 82 smallmouth to 492 mm). The ledger's lead is fully verified.
- Independent federal survey (EPA NRSA 2008, 39 fish taxa) and 19 years of museum vouchers (UA, 35 spp., 1988–2007) omit trout entirely — under the stated standards, broad method-documented lists omitting trout are meaningful negative evidence.
- Summer temperatures (TDEC grabs 22–28.6 °C; the paper's 26–29 °C daily averages June–Sept) exclude even seasonal trout survival past spring; there is no cold-water source in the drainage.
- Zero stocking evidence in any year (2003–2026) or layer (schedule JSON, site master, statewide name sweep); Giles County has never had a TWRA trout destination at all. The nearby stocked waters that could seed confusion are McCutcheon Creek (Maury) and the Tims Ford tailwater (Elk main stem) — neither is Richland Creek.
- Community layer matches: smallmouth/largemouth/green sunfish, float-fishery culture, no trout claims.
- Gaps (non-blocking): 2004–2017 schedules not individually verified; Fishbrain page not opened at water-level; NAS lane rate-limited; the paper itself doesn't name the county (identification is high-confidence but inferential).
- Catalog note: if the catalog currently carries any trout implication for "Richland Creek (Giles/Maury)," it should be corrected to warmwater smallmouth fishery with the Spaulding & Rogers citation; the Elk-system trout association belongs exclusively to the Tims Ford tailwater.
