# Research Log — Big South Fork of the Cumberland River, MAIN STEM (TN + KY)

Water: Big South Fork of the Cumberland River main stem — New River + Clear Fork confluence (Scott Co., TN) north through the gorge (Leatherwood Ford, Angel Falls, Station Camp, O&W, Blue Heron KY, Yamacraw) to Lake Cumberland headwaters (Burnside, KY). Tributaries (Laurel Fork(s), Station Camp Creek, North White Oak, Rock Creek, New River, Clear Fork, Little South Fork) kept separate and flagged.
Question: Are trout documented ON the main stem, or do watershed trout records come from tributaries?
Retrieval date for all sources: 2026-09-24. Research only; no agency/angler contact.

---

  - pp. 104–105 (printed 76–77), Tables 16–17: **TN main stem (KY line to origin, 35 km): DOM, IWS, FAL, REC, LWW, IRR — no TS (trout stream) class. KY main stem (RM 44.3 up, 17 km): WAH (warm water aquatic habitat), PCR, SCR — CAH (cold water) column EMPTY.** Both states: BSF = Outstanding National Resource Water.
  - Appendix D (printed 255–258): "Fishes reported from the Big South Fork watershed upstream of the northernmost BISO boundary in selected studies and NPS unpublished sources 1870–2010." Columns: NPSpecies Dbase / APHN Dbase / Scott 2010 / Evans 1998 / O'Bara et al. 1982 / Brazinski 1979 / Comiskey & Etnier 1972 / Shoup & Peyton 1940 / Kirsch 1893 / Cope 1870. Column totals: 76/78/79/41/44/31/54/34/24/4. Footnote: trout taxa flagged "* Non-native".
  - Appendix D trout rows (x-coordinates extracted with pdfplumber and calibrated against the lake-sturgeon row, which prose says is NPSpecies-only — calibration checks out):
    - **Oncorhynchus mykiss (rainbow trout): NPSpecies, APHN, Scott 2010, O'Bara et al. 1982, Shoup & Peyton 1940** — i.e., the 2003–06 watershed survey did record rainbow trout SOMEWHERE in the watershed (reach not given here; see gap note under B2).
    - **Salmo trutta (brown trout): O'Bara et al. 1982 and Comiskey & Etnier 1972 only** (not Scott 2010).
    - **Salvelinus fontinalis (brook trout): Shoup & Peyton 1940 only** — matches prose "sole report... following hatchery introductions."

Does NOT establish: site-level location of Scott's rainbow record; literal absence.
- Confidence: high (peer-reviewed NPS report; direct table extraction).

- Trout passages (p. 13, Annotated List, Family Salmonidae), quoted:
  - Brown trout: "Salmo trutta Linnaeus—Brown trout. Found at only one locality in the system, the specimen was undoubtedly stocked in the stream by fish and game personnel."
  - "Shoup and Peyton (1940) reported Salvelinus fontinalis and Salmo gairdneri [rainbow trout] from Pickett Lake, a part of the Rock Creek drainage. ## F. SYNTHESIS

1. Surveys that sampled the main stem and reported complete lists WITHOUT trout (weighed by sites/dates/methods/effort):
   - Scott 2010 (NPS/TVA I&M): 19 main-stem samples, ~4,840 individuals, 43–48 spp. per gorge reach, Oct 2003–Nov 2006, multi-gear incl. float runs — no trout in main-stem results; only rainbow trout in the watershed matrix, flagged non-native.
   - Comiskey & Etnier 1968–69 (whole system incl. main-stem gill-net stations): trout = 1 stocked brown, 1 locality; Pickett Lake rainbows/brook = stocked; "not known to occur naturally anywhere in the system."
   - TWRA New River 2010 (headwater fork): 400 fish/20 spp., 26.6 C — no trout; TWRA has no main-stem BSF program 2010–2017.
2. Positive trout records, ALL tributary/lake/stocking-linked: Pickett Lake 1938 (S&P 1940 + GBIF voucher + 2014 iNat); Rock Creek KY (stocked; NAS 1975; iNat 2016, 2024); Little South Fork 1997 (voucher); "S.F. Cumberland tributary" brown 1939 (Kuhne); Laurel Fork of Station Camp Cr "naturally reproducing, upper 3 km" (TDEC, in NRCA Table 20; TDEC 2024 trout-use designation on Laurel Fork per prior pass) — the 2024 TDEC trout designation remains on a TRIBUTARY.
3. The single main-stem trout datapoint anywhere: the 2016 Yamacraw iNat rainbow (LEAD; stocking-stray hypothesis).
4. Regulatory: KY WAH (warmwater) main stem; TN main stem without trout class; ONRW both states; TWRA/KDFWR stock no main-stem trout destination.
5. Community: uniform smallmouth/walleye/rock-bass framing incl. KDFWR and guides; no firsthand main-stem trout catch found.

Recommendation: **WARMWATER-FOCUS** for the BSF main stem (both states), with an explicit footnote that any main-stem trout report (e.g., Yamacraw 2016) is best explained as stray/discard from stocked tributaries (Rock Creek KY; Pickett Lake drainage) — i.e., "seasonal-stocked" applies to the TRIBUTARIES, not the main stem. Not "trout" and not "seasonal-stocked" for the main stem; residual uncertainty is low and narrowing with the Scott report.

Key remaining gap (who holds the settling record): site-level data of Scott 2007/2010 "Fish Survey of BISO" (IRMA DataStore ref 652432; TVA + NPS Appalachian Highlands Network / BISO natural resources staff hold the report and its APHN fish database) — it will pin the one rainbow-trout record to a specific water. Secondary: KDFWR riverine stream survey files for the KY main stem (smallmouth monitoring, Frankfort HQ), not posted online.
